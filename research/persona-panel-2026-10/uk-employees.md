# UK employees driving their own car (n=60)

**Headline:** a weak segment. 43% are on Android and can't install the app. Most of the rest get paid at 45p and file through an employer system.

## Rates
- **Download (iPhone, n=34):** 13 yes (38%), 14 maybe, 7 no. 2 are blocked by MDM.
- **Kept after 2 weeks:** 8 of 27 who tried it (30%).
- **Would pay:** 8 of 60 (13%), almost all yearly. 16 would stay on the free tier.
- **NPS:** −87, mean 4.5. No promoters.

## What sells it
- **The P87 gap** for people paid 0–30p, or a car allowance plus a fuel-only rate (care workers, locums, engineers, pharma/sales reps). For them relief is worth £300–600 a year. Every payer is in this group.
- Automatic logging instead of guessing at month-end.
- Data stays on the phone.

## What kills it
- iPhone only.
- Paid 45p, so the "£ found" total means nothing.
- No export in Concur, Selenity or iTrent format, so everything gets entered twice.
- "Always" location on a personal phone that records patients' addresses.
- 40 free drives runs out mid-month for nurses and midwives.
- Confetti and nags feel gimmicky for work admin.

## Price
Nobody accepted £5.99/£49.99. Payers' ceiling is £3–4 a month (about £30–40 a year). "My employer should pay for this."

## Top feature requests
1. Android version
2. Employer-format export with postcodes, purpose and cost centre
3. P87 summary plus temporary-workplace guidance
4. Privacy mode that hides destinations
5. Rota- and site-aware sorting; import from MileIQ

## Top objections
1. iPhone only
2. Already paid 45p
3. Entering everything twice
4. Tracking on a personal phone / confidentiality
5. Battery on long days; a new solo-founder app

## Quotes
- "Unless this fills in the Trust's system for me it's just a second job." (district nurse, 41)
- "At 25p and 9,000 miles the relief's worth about £500 to me; £50 is a no-brainer." (telecoms engineer, 43)
- "The company pays me 45p and I put it in Concur on a Friday." (area manager, 56)
- "Half our team are on Samsungs, so it's a non-starter." (health visitor, 34)
- "A map of every patient I visit on my own iPhone makes me uneasy." (CPN, 37)
- "Confetti for a tenancy inspection is a bit much." (housing officer, 32)
