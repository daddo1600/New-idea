# US gig drivers: segment summary (n=70)

**Rates.** 23 of 70 (33%) are on Android and can't install it at all. Of all 70: download yes 20 (29%), maybe 22, no 5. Still using after 2 weeks: yes 17 (24%), maybe 16, no 9. Would pay: 14 (20%), 11 yearly and 3 monthly; 22 more are "free only". iPhone only (47): 43% download, 36% keep, 30% pay. Mean NPS 4.4. Net score is -76 overall and -64 on iPhone.

**What sells it.** The missed-miles check, which turns the miles Uber or DoorDash undercounted into dollars. Also the price against MileIQ and Everlance, no account or ads, and the non-English UI.

**What kills it.** iPhone only rules out a third. Stride is free and does more. Gridwise and Solo track earnings. With a shift counted as one drive, full-timers never reach the 40-drive cap, so Pro has little pull.

**Price reaction.** $49.99 a year works only for people leaving MileIQ or Everlance. $5.99 a month is "another subscription". Payers top out at about $5.07 a month. Stride users pay nothing.

**Top 5 feature requests**
1. Android version
2. Earnings tracking ($/mile per app)
3. Expense tracking (gas, phone, tolls)
4. A quarterly estimated tax (1040-ES) amount
5. Cloud backup; Stride/MileIQ import

**Top 5 objections**
1. iPhone only
2. Stride already does more for free
3. Miles alone, with no earnings
4. Logs only on the phone (theft, audit trust)
5. Battery; unproven solo founder

**Quotes**
- "Half the guys in the airport lot run Samsungs, and you built it for the other phone."
- "Stride's free and estimates my taxes; you're charging six bucks for less."
- "Uber said 9 thousand miles, the app found almost 14 thousand, that's real money for me."
- "If a whole block is one drive, I'll never hit 40, so why would I pay?"
- "I'm paying MileIQ fourteen bucks a month for basically this, so fifty a year is an easy switch."
- "I need it to tell me how much to send the IRS in September, not just how many miles."
