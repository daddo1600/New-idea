# Sceptics & edge cases (n=60: GB 17, US 19, CA 12, AU 12)

**Numbers:** 45% would download, 28% maybe; 20% can't (Android). 28% are still using it after 2 weeks. 42% would pay (8 monthly, 17 yearly), but their median ceiling is about 5 in local currency, below list price. NPS −70.

**What sells it:** no account and on-device storage. The missed-miles check wins couriers. Undercutting MileIQ on price helps, and so does light battery use.

**What kills it:**
- iPhone only.
- "Always" location gets revoked and logging stops without warning.
- Swiping doesn't work with VoiceOver or a tremor.
- Some users don't fit the rates: cycling, trucking, W-2 jobs, charity driving.
- "£X found" reads as cash.
- Trials that auto-renew.

**What would flip sceptics:** a backup they control, a privacy audit, battery data, a serious mode, tap-to-sort, a MileIQ/Driversnote import, Android, and an alert when permissions are lost.

**Accountants:** "Rates right, workflow wrong." 2 of 8 would recommend it now. They need:
- client sharing
- a locked edit history
- cloud backup
- Xero, QuickBooks and FreeAgent formats
- MTD quarterly summaries
- the ATO logbook method
- the tax saving shown, not the deduction

**Top requests:**
1. Android
2. ATO logbook method
3. Sharing with an accountant and accounting exports
4. Backup and audit trail
5. Accessibility

**Top objections:**
1. iPhone only
2. No backup, no track record
3. Misleading "found" figure and childish gamification
4. Rates don't fit (bike, truck, 5,000 km cap, Quebec)
5. Subscription distrust; free tier is enough

**Quotes:**
- "If my phone goes in a lake I lose a year of evidence."
- "With VoiceOver a swipe right goes to the next element, not 'business'."
- "Ninety percent of PCO drivers I know use Android."
- "If a client can rewrite March in December with no edit history, it's weaker in an audit."
- "A quarterly mileage summary would make this my default recommendation for every courier client."
- "'Found £2,217' when it's a deduction worth a fraction of that is the line I'd quote critically."
