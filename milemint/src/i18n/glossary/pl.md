# Polish (pl) glossary

Audience: Polish drivers, couriers and tradespeople living in the UK. The text is plain, everyday standard Polish with full diacritics. It avoids regional slang, keeps UK and other tax terms in English as the brief asks, and leaves some UK words that Poles in the UK use every day ("van", "Self Assessment").

## Form of address

- The app speaks to the user informally as **Ty** (2nd person singular: "Stuknij", "Oznacz", "Twoje przejazdy"), as modern Polish consumer apps and Apple's Polish iOS do. "Pan/Pani" would sound stiff and official for an everyday app.
- Possessive and personal pronouns that address the user are **capitalised** (Twój, Twoje, Twojej, Ci, Ciebie) everywhere, mid-sentence too. Final decision (check 3): keep it. Apple's Polish iOS writes them this way (the app quotes iOS wording and sits next to its alerts), and so do Polish banks and delivery apps. It reads polite without sounding formal. All lines are consistent; no lowercase forms remain.
- **Gender-neutral wording.** Polish past tense and many adjectives mark gender ("jechałeś/jechałaś", "gotowy/gotowa"). Every line is phrased so it works for anyone: present or future tense ("oznaczę", "zaparkujesz"), impersonal forms ("jak wyliczono Twoje kilometry", "znaleziono"), or nouns instead of adjectives ("Zawsze w gotowości").
- Share messages the user sends are in the first person and also gender-neutral ("Dzięki MileMint mam już…", "które przepadłyby mi"). "New to Pro?" is "Pierwszy raz z Pro?", which avoids "Nowy/Nowa".
- MileMint is treated as masculine ("MileMint znalazł", "MileMint zapisał").

## Typography and numbers

- Polish quotation marks „…” are used around quoted buttons and examples.
- Thousands are written the Polish way: "5000 km", "10 000 mil", "75 000 $", "50 000 £".
- Dates: "31 stycznia", "15 kwietnia" (day first, month in the genitive).
- Durations read "2 h 05 min".
- Decimals use a comma, including the input example "12,5" (the distance field accepts a decimal comma).
- Short weekday labels are the standard two-letter forms: Pn, Wt, Śr, Cz, Pt, So, Nd.

## Adapted jokes and greetings

- "Knock knock / Who's there?" became the Polish "Puk, puk / Kto tam?" format, which works the same way.
- "Swipe right on savings" / "Easiest date of the week": kept the dating-app idea ("Przesuń w prawo, to może być miłość" / "Najprostsza randka tygodnia"), as Tinder's swipe right is widely known in Poland. Not "najłatwiejsza": "łatwa" next to "randka" hints at "łatwa dziewczyna".
- "Sunday scaries" became "Niedzielna chandra" (Sunday blues).
- "Shoebox of receipts" became "karton paragonów".
- "Every penny" became "Zasłużone w stu procentach", so no currency is named.
- "Spring has sprung" became "Przyszła wiosna"; "G’day!" became a casual "Cześć!".
- "Autumn/Fall miles add up" and "Sunny days, business miles" use "trasy" (routes) instead of miles, so they read well for km countries too.
- Shift cheers: "Ruszamy!", "No to jedziemy!", "Czas ruszać!", "Do dzieła!", "Działamy!", "W drogę!".
- Money-flavoured reminders are kept honest: "Free money alert" → "Odliczenia czekają", "Plot twist: driving pays" → "Zwrot akcji: jazda się liczy", "Low effort, high reward" → "Mało wysiłku, duży efekt". No "darmowe pieniądze", "zgarnij", "odzyskaj" or "nagroda".
- "(slightly cheeky)" → "z przymrużeniem oka" (not "zaczepne", which means combative).
- Tagline-style lines shown in every country don't name a unit: "Never miss a mile" → "Żaden przejazd nie przepadnie", "Every business mile, counted" → "Każdy służbowy przejazd policzony"; the share lines likewise say "każdy przejazd".
- Avoided double meanings: "Pierwszy raz z Pro?" → "Jeszcze nie masz Pro?"; "Pierwsza zmiana zaliczona" → "Pierwsza zmiana za Tobą".
