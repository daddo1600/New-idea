# Polish (pl) glossary

Audience: Polish drivers, couriers and tradespeople living in the UK. The text is plain, everyday standard Polish with full diacritics. It avoids regional slang, keeps UK and other tax terms in English as the brief asks, and leaves some UK words that Poles in the UK use every day ("van", "Self Assessment").

## Form of address

- The app speaks to the user informally as **Ty** (2nd person singular: "Stuknij", "Oznacz", "Twoje przejazdy"), as modern Polish consumer apps and Apple's Polish iOS do. "Pan/Pani" would sound stiff and official for an everyday app.
- Possessive and personal pronouns that address the user are **capitalised** (Twój, Twoje, Twojej, Ci, Ciebie). This is the usual polite convention in Polish written to one reader, and many Polish apps follow it ("Twoje konto").
- **Gender-neutral wording.** Polish past tense and many adjectives mark gender ("jechałeś/jechałaś", "gotowy/gotowa"). Every line is phrased so it works for anyone: present or future tense ("oznaczę", "zaparkujesz"), impersonal forms ("jak wyliczono Twoje kilometry", "znaleziono"), or nouns instead of adjectives ("Zawsze w gotowości").
- Share messages the user sends are in the first person and also gender-neutral ("Dzięki MileMint mam już…", "które przepadłyby mi"). "New to Pro?" is "Pierwszy raz z Pro?", which avoids "Nowy/Nowa".
- MileMint is treated as masculine ("MileMint znalazł", "MileMint zapisał").

## Typography and numbers

- Polish quotation marks „…” are used around quoted buttons and examples.
- Thousands are written the Polish way: "5000 km", "10 000 mil", "75 000 $", "50 000 £".
- Dates: "31 stycznia", "15 kwietnia" (day first, month in the genitive).
- Durations read "2 h 05 min".
- **Exception:** the input example stays "12.5" (with a dot), because the distance field only accepts a decimal point (see Unsure).
- Short weekday labels are the standard two-letter forms: Pn, Wt, Śr, Cz, Pt, So, Nd.

## Terms

| English | Polish | Why |
|---|---|---|
| drive / trip | przejazd (1 przejazd, 2 przejazdy, 5 przejazdów, 1,5 przejazdu) | One word for both, so the app reads consistently. "Podróż" sounds like a holiday, "kurs" like a taxi ride. |
| business (trip type) | służbowy (przejazd służbowy, mile/km służbowe), "służbowo" | The standard Polish word for work-related travel ("podróż służbowa", "samochód służbowy"). Not "biznesowy", which sounds like a company. |
| personal | prywatny | Standard opposite of "służbowy". |

A short Polish gloss is added in brackets where it helps (e.g. "Mileage Allowance Relief (ulgę za przebieg)", "logbook" after "rejestr").

## Adapted jokes and greetings

- "Knock knock / Who's there?" became the Polish "Puk, puk / Kto tam?" format, which works the same way.
- "Swipe right on savings" / "Easiest date of the week": kept the dating-app idea ("Przesuń w prawo, to może być miłość" / "Najłatwiejsza randka w tym tygodniu"), as Tinder's swipe right is widely known in Poland.
- "Sunday scaries" became "Niedzielna chandra" (Sunday blues).
- "Shoebox of receipts" became "karton paragonów".
- "Every penny" became "Zasłużone w stu procentach", so no currency is named.
- "Spring has sprung" became "Przyszła wiosna"; "G’day!" became a casual "Cześć!".
- "Autumn/Fall miles add up" and "Sunny days, business miles" use "trasy" (routes) instead of miles, so they read well for km countries too.
- Shift cheers: "Ruszamy!", "No to jedziemy!", "Czas ruszać!", "Do dzieła!", "Dasz radę!", "W drogę!".

## Unsure

1. **iOS wording (all of the table above).** I'm fairly sure of "Pozwalaj, gdy używana", "Zawsze", "Nigdy", "Lokalizacja", "Ustawienia → Powiadomienia". Please check on a Polish iPhone: "Zmień na Zawsze pozwalaj", "Podczas używania aplikacji" (it may be "Gdy używam aplikacji"), "Zapytaj następnym razem lub gdy udostępniam", "Otwórz Ustawienia" (the alert button may just say "Ustawienia"), and the section header "POZWÓL NA DOSTĘP DO LOKALIZACJI" (it may be "ZEZWALAJ…").
2. **Decimal comma (app issue, not a translation issue).** "Wpisz przejechane kilometry, np. 12.5." keeps a dot because `parseMiles` in `src/domain/format.ts` only accepts "12.5". A Polish iPhone's decimal keypad types a comma ("12,5"), which the parser would reject. The parser should probably accept a comma too.
3. **"{{counted}} mil" in the share messages** ("Moja aplikacja kurierska naliczyła … {{counted}} mil", "{{logged}} mil służbowych", "{{extra}} mil"). These numbers have no plural forms, so I used the genitive plural "mil", which is right for 0, 5–21, 25+ etc., but 2–4, 22–24 etc. would need "mile". The km versions don't have this problem.
4. **"Kept, locked" → "Zapisane, zablokowane"** and **"Best value" → "Najkorzystniej"**: short table/badge texts. Please check they fit.
5. **"UK" → "Wlk. Brytania"** in the half-width country tile is much longer than "UK". Many Poles in the UK say "UK", so "UK" may also be fine if space is tight.
6. **"Auto: …" → "Autom.: …"** for the auto-classification notes, because "Auto" in Polish also means "car" ("Auto lub van"), which could be confusing.
7. **"Go Pro" → "Kup Pro"** (short header button). "Upgrade to Pro" and "Go Pro any time" use "Przejdź na Pro".
8. **Length.** Polish is naturally about 30–60% longer than English. Some small labels go over 1.3× (e.g. "Terminy podatkowe", "Odzyskane pieniądze", "Spróbuj ponownie", "Zakończ zmianę"); I couldn't make them shorter without losing clarity. Please check them on screen.
9. **"{{label}}: zdobyte / jeszcze nie"** (screen-reader badge state). Fine with most titles, but the agreement is neutral rather than matched to each milestone's gender.
10. **Capitalised "Twój/Ci"**: a deliberate style choice. If the team prefers lowercase (also common in apps), it's a simple search-and-replace.
