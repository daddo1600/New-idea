# Gig driver mileage-app sentiment outside Reddit: Facebook and creator content

**Method note (read first):** Research done 2026-10-01 using web search only. Direct page fetches were blocked by the network egress proxy for facebook-adjacent and creator sites (therideshareguy.com, ridesharingdriver.com, entrecourier.com, gridwise.io, worksolo.com, youtube.com, vice.com, cnn.com). So the findings below come from **search-engine snippets and summaries**, not from reading full pages. No verbatim Facebook post or YouTube comment text could be retrieved. Treat quoted phrasing as search-snippet paraphrase unless marked otherwise, and re-check key claims in a browser before using them externally. Facebook group contents were not viewed. Where a group is not confirmed public, it is listed as "status not verified" rather than guessed.

## 1. Which Facebook groups/pages are the main gathering places, and are they public or private?

### Takeaway
Only a few named, citable Facebook communities turned up in search. The best-confirmed one is the **"Deliveroo Riders UK" page (~47.9k followers)**. Amazon Flex driver groups are known to be mostly **closed/private**, and there are "dozens" of them across the US, UK and Spain. Search surfaced a large number of public **black-market account-rental groups** (80 groups, up to 800k members combined) more readily than genuine rider-community groups. Search engines do not index the content of private groups, so mileage and tax discussion inside them cannot be seen.

### Cited Findings
- **UK – "Deliveroo Riders UK"** (Facebook page, facebook.com/deliverooridersUK): about **47,868 followers**. It describes itself as a Deliveroo rider community offering "advice, competitions, events, partnerships and discounts". It is a public page. No mileage or tax posts were visible through search. — [Facebook: Deliveroo Riders UK](https://www.facebook.com/deliverooridersUK/)
- **Amazon Flex (US/UK/Spain):** Leaked internal Amazon reports (VICE, Sept 2020) showed that Amazon's "Advocacy Operations Social Listening Team" watched "dozens of closed driver Facebook pages" for Flex drivers in the US, UK and Spain. The reports captured drivers' full names and posts about complaints and labour actions. This confirms that the main Flex gathering places are **closed/private groups**. — [VICE (via search summary)](https://vice.com/en/article/3azegw/amazon-is-spying-on-its-workers-in-closed-facebook-groups-internal-reports-show); [ClassAction.org on resulting lawsuit](https://www.classaction.org/news/orwellian-lawsuit-says-amazon-wiretapping-flex-drivers-private-facebook-groups-to-track-union-efforts-labor-strife); [Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/privacy/amazon-flex-drivers-allege-company-snoops-on-private-facebook-groups/)
- **Amazon Flex public group:** A search summary pointed to a public "Amazon Flex" Facebook group at facebook.com/groups/1777497459204920/ and mentioned an "Amazon Flex Accounting Group". Neither the group's name, its size nor its public status could be verified directly. — [Facebook group link](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1777497459204920/) (status not verified)
- **Black-market account groups (US/UK/AU):** The Tech Transparency Project found **80 Facebook groups**, with up to 800,000 members, where people buy, sell and rent Uber, DoorDash and Deliveroo driver accounts. Examples: "UBER ACCOUNT FOR RENT WORLDWIDE" (public, more than 22,000 members) and "Uber Delivery Drivers Account for Rent" (created 2024, more than 2,000 members). These groups are noise, not a target audience. — [CNN, Apr 14 2025](https://www.cnn.com/2025/04/14/tech/facebook-groups-buy-sell-uber-doordash-deliveroo-accounts); [ABC17 syndication](https://abc17news.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2025/04/14/incredibly-concerning-facebook-black-market-groups-offer-rideshare-and-delivery-driver-accounts-for-sale-researchers-say/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/04/15/meta-uber-facebook-black-market-groups/)
- **Canada – Punjabi community:** The only Punjabi/Canadian driver presence that search turned up was a trucking page, "Punjabi-Canadian Truck Drivers & Owner Operators" (a news/blog page about the truck industry). No Brampton or Toronto Uber Eats/DoorDash courier group was found. — [Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/p/Punjabi-Canadian-Truck-Drivers-Owner-Operators-100065564136314/)
- **Australia:** Search found a "DeliveryTasker" Facebook page (facebook.com/DeliveryTaskForce). Its size and focus could not be verified. No Uber Eats or DoorDash rider group for Sydney or Melbourne was found. Context: Menulog stopped operating in Australia on 26 Nov 2025, so the main platforms there are now Uber Eats, DoorDash and Deliveroo. — [Facebook: DeliveryTasker](https://www.facebook.com/DeliveryTaskForce); [Wikipedia: Menulog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menulog)

### Inferences
- The biggest communities (DoorDash Dasher groups, Uber Eats driver groups, Amazon Flex groups, city-level groups) are almost certainly private. Amazon's own monitoring of "dozens of closed" Flex groups supports this. For MileMint, Facebook is a channel for **organic seeding by members or admins**. It is not a source of scrapeable sentiment.
- Facebook groups are heavily polluted by account-rental scams. Any community-marketing push there needs to stay clear of that association.

### Gaps
- No member counts were obtained for US groups (e.g. "DoorDash Dashers", "Uber Eats Drivers"), Canadian groups (Brampton/GTA couriers), or Australian groups (Uber Eats/DoorDash riders). Facebook search requires a login, and fetches were blocked.
- No publicly visible mileage or tax posts from any Facebook group were retrievable. **No verbatim Facebook quotes are available.**

## 2. What do creators and gig-driver blogs say about Stride, Gridwise, Everlance, MileIQ, Driversnote, Solo and similar apps?

### Takeaway
Creator and blog verdicts split along incentive lines. **The Rideshare Guy (RSG)** calls Stride good and free. Independent tester **EntreCourier** (Ron Shrewsbury) is harsher: Stride gets a **D+**, and Solo **missed 51% of trips** in his test. Much of the "best mileage app" content online is published by the app vendors themselves (Gridwise, Solo, Everlance), each ranking its own app first. Recurring criticisms across apps are missed trips, battery drain, manual start/stop, and features locked behind paywalls.

### Cited Findings
- **RSG on Stride:** RSG's team says Stride is "a good app; it's well-designed and intuitive to use", is free with unlimited trips, and that "many on the RSG team also like Stride". (Snippet; page title "11 Best Mileage Tracker Apps of 2026".) — [The Rideshare Guy](https://therideshareguy.com/day-5-what-are-the-best-apps-to-track-your-mileage/); [RSG Stride review](https://therideshareguy.com/stride-tax-app-mileage-tracker/)
- **RSG on Solo:** RSG has a "Solo Gig App Review 2026". A search summary says RSG named Solo a top pick for mileage tracking. — [RSG Solo review](https://therideshareguy.com/solo-app-review/)
- **EntreCourier on Stride:** grade **D+** ("Stride App Review 2023 (Mileage Tracker Report Card)"). The verdict is "free… easy to use, however many features are extremely limited". The review says Stride has no automatic mileage tracking and no upgrade option that adds it, and that being "too easy to use can be a problem". Mileage and expenses count double in his grading. Note that this contradicts claims elsewhere that Stride auto-tracks, so Stride may have added auto-tracking after 2023. — [EntreCourier Stride review](https://entrecourier.com/delivery/delivery-strategies/delivery-tools/stride-tax-app-review-2021/)
- **EntreCourier on Solo:** Solo links to driver accounts and auto-classifies trips as business or personal. However, EntreCourier reports that Solo "has problems with missing trips and doesn't provide all the trip information required by the IRS", and that it **missed 51% of trips** in his test. Pricing cited: free tier, and a Basic plan at $3.99/mo for mileage, expenses and tax estimator. — [EntreCourier Solo review](https://entrecourier.com/delivery/delivery-strategies/delivery-tools/solo-app-review-mileage-tracking/)
- **Gridwise (vendor) on itself vs Stride:** Gridwise's own comparison says Stride is "best for casual or budget-conscious drivers… who do not mind manual start/stop". It positions Gridwise as best for multi-app drivers because it connects Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Instacart, Grubhub and Amazon Flex accounts to pull in earnings automatically. **This is vendor content.** — [Gridwise vs Everlance vs Stride (2026)](https://gridwise.io/blog/gridwise-vs-everlance-vs-stride); [Gridwise vs Stride](https://gridwise.io/blog/rideshare/gridwise-vs-stride-which-works-better/)
- **Vendor-authored "best app" lists are everywhere:** Gridwise ([best mileage tracker 2026](https://gridwise.io/blog/best-mileage-tracker-app)), Solo ([7 best mileage trackers 2026](https://www.worksolo.com/blog/best-mileage-tracker-apps-for-gig-workers-in-2026); [Solo vs Gridwise](https://www.worksolo.com/blog/solo-vs-gridwise)), Everlance ([Gridwise alternatives](https://www.everlance.com/alternatives/gridwise)), plus newer entrants (ShiftTracker, MileClear, MileTrack, TruMile) all publish SEO comparisons.
- **Recurring complaints, via an aggregator of driver reviews:** Battery drain is the most common complaint; the aggregator cites one driver whose phone turned into "a toaster by 3pm" on Stride. Missed trips and impossible routes "cost real tax deductions". Everlance "sometimes over-logged personal trips and missed short urban delivery hops". An Everlance user is quoted: "sometimes the tracking classification is logged and accurate, and sometimes it is missed. The user needs to check after each trip". MileIQ draws accuracy complaints, with one claim that it "misses at least 20% of miles". **Caveat:** these come from competitor sites (TruMile, Zeo, Timeero) that summarise Reddit and app-store reviews. They are not primary quotes. — [TruMile "Best mileage tracker according to Reddit"](https://trumile.app/compare/best-mileage-tracker-reddit/); [Zeo](https://zeorouteplanner.com/best-mileage-tracking-apps/); [Timeero Stride review](https://timeero.com/reviews/stride-review)
- **UK (Driversnote):** UK sole-trader guides describe Driversnote as UK-friendly and say it handles HMRC reporting well. It offers GPS auto-detect, optional Bluetooth beacons and team features. The same guides state that the HMRC AMAP rate rose from 45p to **55p/mile for the first 10,000 miles from 6 April 2026** (25p after that). This rate change was reported by an app-vendor guide and should be checked against gov.uk. — [Startup Edit](https://www.startupedit.co.uk/guides/mileage-tracker-apps/); [MileClear](https://mileclear.com/delivery-driver-mileage-tracker); [MileTrack UK test](https://miletrack.app/en-gb/blog/best-mileage-tracker-app-uk/)
- **UK creators:** The prominent UK courier YouTube content found is earnings-focused, not tax-focused. "London Eats" (Milo Sterlini) films Deliveroo, Uber Eats and Just Eat shifts and shows earnings at the end of each video (e.g. £62.22 for 5 hrs, i.e. £12.44/hr). Other examples are videos such as "I Made £30 an Hour as a UberEats Rider in London!" and "I Became A Deliveroo Rider For A Week and Made £___". No UK creator mileage-app recommendation was found. — [Yahoo News UK](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/how-much-delivery-riders-earn-uk-deliveroo-uber-eats-164924654.html); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=juseDkZf-E4); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ua4cwQ-0Oxg)
- **TikTok:** TikTok has auto-generated discover hubs for "how to track mileage for DoorDash using Stride app", "best app to track mileage DoorDash" and "how to use Stride app for taxes". This shows that Stride is the default app name in US DoorDash TikTok tax content. A recurring creator framing is that mileage is "your largest tax write-off as a 1099 DoorDash driver, and missing it can cost you thousands". — [TikTok discover: Stride](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/how-to-track-mileage-for-doordash-using-stride-app); [TikTok discover: best app](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/best-app-to-track-mileage-doordash); [TikTok discover: Stride taxes](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/how-to-use-stride-app-for-taxes)
- **Canada:** The content is accountant-led rather than creator-led. Sources stress that the CRA needs a trip-level log (date, start, destination, purpose, km) and that vehicle expenses can be "completely denied" in an audit without one. They also note that platforms report income to the CRA from 2025 above 30 activities or CA$2,800. Instaccountant cites $0.73/km for the first 5,000 km in 2026; that is the CRA allowance rate, and self-employed drivers generally deduct actual costs pro-rated by business km. — [Instaccountant 2026](https://instaccountant.com/2026-cra-tax-changes-for-uber-drivers-canada/); [LawyerInfo CRA audits](https://lawyerinfo.ca/guides/money-taxes-ip/cra-tax-audits-for-uber-and-rideshare-drivers-in-canada/)

### Inferences
- The one independent tester (EntreCourier) rates the incumbents poorly on accuracy. That suggests a "verifiably accurate, IRS/HMRC/CRA-compliant log" positioning is credible and under-served.
- Stride's "free" status makes it the default on TikTok. A paid MileMint will need a clear free tier or a clear accuracy advantage to be recommended.
- In the UK, Canada and Australia, creator content concentrates on earnings rather than tax. There is a gap for country-specific mileage-tax content: the UK's MTD (Making Tax Digital) rules and the 55p rate, the CRA logbook rules in Canada, and the ATO cents-per-km method in Australia.

### Gaps
- No YouTube comment text or video-specific creator verdicts could be retrieved, because YouTube was blocked. Named US YouTubers beyond RSG were not confirmed for this topic.
- No Australian or Canadian delivery creators commenting on mileage apps were found.
- No creator commentary on Para or MileIQ specifically was found, beyond aggregator complaints.

## 3. Sponsorship and affiliate relationships, and how drivers share tips

### Takeaway
Sponsorship is pervasive. RSG openly runs on affiliate revenue, and Solo runs a creator affiliate program ("Friends of Solo") that lists RSG among its affiliates. Gridwise runs an affiliate page and sells partnership access to its driver audience. Earnings-sharing is a core content format: shift-end earnings reveals on YouTube, and screenshot-style posts. That makes shareable earnings and mileage summary cards a natural fit for MileMint's referral loop.

### Cited Findings
- **RSG disclosure:** TheRideshareGuy.com is part of affiliate networks, and "many rideshare and delivery companies whose products are listed on the site pay referral fees". Compensation "may impact how and where products appear on the site". — [RSG Advertising Policy](https://therideshareguy.com/disclaimer/)
- **Solo creator affiliate program:** "Friends of Solo" is billed as "The Best Affiliate Program for Gig Economy Creators". A search summary says The Rideshare Guy is listed among its affiliates. This could not be verified directly because the page fetch was blocked. — [Solo: Friends of Solo](https://www.worksolo.com/friends-of-solo)
- **Gridwise:** Gridwise runs an affiliate landing page and a "Partner with Gridwise to reach and engage gig workers" page. It also has a weekly podcast that claims a "100,000 strong driver community". A search summary asserted a LinkedIn link between RSG's owner and Gridwise; this is **unverified** and should not be repeated without checking. — [Gridwise affiliate](https://gridwise.io/affiliate-fall/); [Gridwise partnerships](https://gridwise.io/partnerships); [Gridwise podcast](https://creators.spotify.com/pod/profile/gridwise/)
- **Everlance** runs a platform-partnership program for expense tracking (B2B rather than creators). — [Everlance](https://www.everlance.com/blog/why-platforms-should-partner-with-everlance-for-expense-tracking)
- **Earnings-sharing culture:** UK creator "London Eats" ends each video with that shift's earnings. Title formats such as "I Made £30 an Hour…" and "Made £___" are common. — [Yahoo News UK](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/how-much-delivery-riders-earn-uk-deliveroo-uber-eats-164924654.html); [YouTube example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=juseDkZf-E4)

### Inferences
- Many "best app" recommendations from creators are affiliate-driven, so drivers have reason to discount them. A transparent, non-paid accuracy test, like EntreCourier's grading method, may carry more trust than sponsorship. Creator affiliate deals are still the standard acquisition channel in this market, and MileMint would compete with Solo and Gridwise for the same creators.
- Shareable assets that mirror the earnings-reveal format could plug into existing sharing habits. Examples are a weekly "miles + deduction saved" card, or a "£/hr after mileage" figure. Since many drivers post in private Facebook groups, a share card that reads well as a screenshot matters more than link previews.

### Gaps
- Specific sponsored videos (e.g. "this video is sponsored by Gridwise/Solo/Everlance") could not be confirmed because YouTube was blocked.
- There is no direct evidence on how drivers post mileage totals (as opposed to earnings) in Facebook groups.
- Fees and payouts for the Solo and Gridwise affiliate programs were not retrieved.
