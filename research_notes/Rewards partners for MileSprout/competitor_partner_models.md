# Competitor Partner, Rewards and Revenue Models (mileage-tracking and gig-driver apps)

Research date: 2 Oct 2026. Method note: much of this comes from search-result summaries. The egress proxy blocked direct fetches of gridwise.io, prnewswire.com, newswire.com, wikipedia.org and insurancebusinessmag.com, so I could not read those primary pages in full. Each item carries its source URL. Anything dated before 2024 is marked **[pre-2024]**. Figures from aggregators (Tracxn, Owler, Discoperi, comparison blogs) are marked **[aggregator, unverified]**.

## Q1. Which apps run partner offers or rewards marketplaces, with which named partners, and what deal structures?

### Takeaway
The strongest examples of partner-funded "free" models in this space are Gridwise (benefits store plus paid Plus tier plus a data business) and Stride (commission-based insurance brokerage). Both mix in other revenue, and neither relies on offers alone. Mileage-first apps (Everlance, MileIQ, Driversnote, Hurdlr) earn mainly from subscriptions and B2B. Their "partnerships" mostly run the other way: the app is the perk distributed through someone else's rewards programme (Lyft, Texas REALTORS), or it is embedded or white-labelled in banks (Hurdlr with Nymbus and Locality Bank).

- Freemium plus a paid tier. Gridwise Plus costs $14.99/month or $107.99/year. Plus users get up to $15,000 of no-cost insurance (including $10,000 life cover, $2,500 critical illness and $1,000 medical-expense cash) through Avibra. Avibra's own benefit plans cost from $1/week — [Gridwise benefits page](https://gridwise.io/benefits); [Gridwise Dollar Benefits](https://gridwise.io/dollar-benefits/); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gridwise-gig-driver-assistant/id1215991382)

This is an embedded insurtech marketplace: Gridwise is the distributor and Avibra is the carrier/MGA front end. The commercial split is not disclosed **[pre-2024, approx. Jan 2022]** — [PR Newswire: Gridwise–Avibra](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/gig-drivers-have-access-to-affordable-wellness-benefits-for-the-first-time-ever-thanks-to-gridwiseavibra-partnership-301467152.html); [Flexible Work News](https://flexibleworknews.com/stories/623749977-avibra-and-gridwise-offer-affordable-access-to-benefits-for-gig-drivers-drivers-have-been-stuck-in-limbo)

### Inferences
- Deal structures seen: (a) **insurance brokerage commission / embedded insurance** (Stride, Gridwise–Avibra, Qover, Catch); (b) **affiliate/CPA cashback** (Upside); (c) **distribution-as-perk**, where a platform or association subsidises the tool (Lyft–Everlance, Texas REALTORS); (d) **white-label/embedded in banks** (Hurdlr–Nymbus/Locality, Ember–Starling); (e) **data licensing** (Gridwise Analytics). No pure "offers-only" mileage tracker appears to have scaled.
- Gig platforms (Uber, Deliveroo, Lyft) already give drivers fuel, maintenance and insurance perks for free. A third-party app's offers must therefore beat or complement these, for example by stacking with them or covering multi-apping couriers who don't reach the higher Uber Pro tiers.

### Gaps
- Commission or CPA rates for Avibra, Upside and Stride were not disclosed publicly.
- I found no evidence that TripLog, QuickBooks mileage, Keeper, Moonlighting, Coconut, Untied or Pie run a consumer offers marketplace. Not verified due to time and fetch blocks.
- I found nothing on UK courier community apps' monetisation.

## Q2. Public numbers: revenue, users, revenue per user, funding, acquisitions, shutdowns

### Takeaway
2025–26 was a consolidation period. Everlance went to Motus (Feb 2025), Ember to Starling (Aug 2025), Stride to Integrity (Jul 2026) and MileIQ reportedly to Bending Spoons (2025). Para shut down in 2024 after platform cease-and-desist letters. Subscription mileage apps report real ARR (Driversnote about €10M, Everlance about $16.5M). No benefits or offers app disclosed revenue per user.

### Inferences
- Exits went to strategic buyers who want distribution: Motus (B2B reimbursement), Starling (bank customers), Integrity (insurance distribution). This suggests the audience is valuable mainly to insurance and finance distributors. For MileSprout, banks and insurers are the most likely long-term partners or acquirers.
- Catch and Stride show that insurance commissions can sustain a free app, but only at large scale (millions of users, 140 platform partners). Even Stride ultimately sold to a distributor. Para shows that relying on platform credentials is fragile, which favours MileSprout's GPS-only on-device approach.
- MileIQ's reported price rises (if accurate) create a switching opportunity for a free app.

### Gaps
- No public revenue per user for Stride, Gridwise or Catch. Gridwise revenue and user counts after 2022 were not found.
- Hurdlr's current ownership status is unconfirmed: no 2024–26 acquisition was found.
- No layoffs data was found for these companies.

## Q3. How offers are presented in-app without eroding trust

### Takeaway
The public patterns are: frame offers as "benefits" (not ads), bundle a no-cost core benefit as the hook, tie deeper discounts to loyalty tiers, and state that recommendations are commission-blind. I found little public evidence on placement or frequency.

### Cited Findings
- Stride says its engine is commission-blind and recommends the plan that saves the user most "even if Stride doesn't get paid". It states that commissions are built into the premium, so users pay no extra — [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/stride-health-coverage-easier-gig-economy) **[pre-2024]**
- Gridwise uses a dedicated "Benefits" / "Dollar Benefits" section, with no-cost insurance included for Plus subscribers as an anchor benefit — [Gridwise benefits](https://gridwise.io/benefits)
- Lyft and Uber tie perk depth to performance tiers (Lyft × Everlance discount from 20% up to free; Uber Pro Blue → Diamond) — [Everlance blog](https://www.everlance.com/blog/everlance-lyft-partnership); [Ridester](https://www.ridester.com/uber-pro/)
- Deliveroo presents perks on a separate rider "perks" page that requires sign-up, rather than in the delivery flow — [Deliveroo perks](https://rider.deliveroo.co.uk/perks)

### Inferences
- For MileSprout this suggests a separate, opt-in "Perks" tab, a short "how we earn" disclosure (Stride-style), and no offers interrupting the tracking or report flows.

### Gaps
- No public data on offer click-through rates, frequency caps, or user backlash. App Store review mining was not done.

## Q4. Selling or licensing driver data, and how on-device privacy differentiates MileSprout

### Takeaway
Gridwise openly sells anonymised, aggregated driver data (location, trips, earnings) to cities, airports, retailers, financial institutions and investors, including through Exabel's investor platform. This is a clear point of contrast for a privacy-first, on-device app.

### Cited Findings
- Gridwise "anonymizes all data before sharing it with or selling it to third parties". Its buyers include cities, airports, retail organisations and financial institutions — [Gridwise privacy policy](https://gridwise.io/privacypolicy/); [Gridwise data use](https://gridwise.io/data-use)
- Gridwise Analytics "ingests and processes millions of anonymized location, trip, and earnings records" from drivers on Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, Grubhub, Instacart, Amazon Flex and others. It sells reports and an investor offering — [Gridwise Analytics](https://gridwise.io/analytics); [Gridwise Reports](https://gridwise.io/analytics/solutions/gridwise-reports/); [Investor insights blog](https://gridwise.io/blog/analytics/understanding-gig-drivers-investor-insights-from-gridwise-analytics/)
- Gridwise data is distributed to investors via **Exabel** (alternative-data platform) — [Exabel changelog](https://help.exabel.com/changelog/gridwise-insights-platform)
- The 2022 funding was explicitly to "rapidly scale its enterprise data analytics business" **[pre-2024]** — [Crosslink](https://www.crosslinkcapital.com/news/gridwise-raises-new-funds-in-sign-vcs-still-have-faith-in-gig-worker-economy/)
- Upside earns most of its revenue from affiliate commissions from a large merchant network. Card-linked offers like this need transaction data — [Upside partnerships](https://www.upside.com/partnerships)

### Inferences
- MileSprout can position itself against Gridwise as "your trips never leave your phone, and are never sold". Card-linked offers (Upside-style) and insurance quotes, however, need some user data shared with the partner. The privacy promise therefore has to be built as user-initiated, per-offer consent, with the partner receiving data only when the user clicks.

### Gaps
- I found nothing showing that Everlance, MileIQ or Driversnote sell aggregate data. Their privacy policies were not reviewed.
