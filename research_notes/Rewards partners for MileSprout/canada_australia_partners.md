# Canada and Australia rewards/affiliate partners for MileSprout

All sources accessed 2026-10-02.

**Method caveat (read first).** The research proxy blocked direct page fetches (WebFetch) for most brand domains (wealthsimple.com, xero.com, koho.ca, rates.ca, lowestrates.ca, myzeller.com, airwallex.com). Most findings below therefore come from **search-engine result summaries** of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Payout figures from third-party directories (getlasso.co, linkclicky.com, uppromote.com, affiliate-toolkit.com, etc.) are aggregator claims. Treat them as indicative, and confirm them inside the network dashboard before relying on them. Payouts in affiliate networks are often negotiated per publisher and change frequently.

**Contacts policy applied.** No email addresses were guessed or constructed. Where a search snippet showed an email only as Cloudflare-obfuscated "[email protected]" (LowestRates.ca), the address is NOT reproduced. The note says it is published on the brand's contact page. No named affiliate managers were found in a verifiable professional-role source within this pass. Default where no contact was found: "apply via programme page".

## Q8. Compliance basics

### Takeaway
- **AU credit:** referring users to a credit product can be done without a credit licence only under the narrow **referrer exemption**. MileSprout may only say that a licensee can help, give contact details, and disclose any commission, and must not charge the consumer.
- **AU financial products and insurance:** recommendations are "general advice" requiring an AFSL or authorised-representative status plus a general advice warning (RG 244). Pure factual information and links are lower-risk.
- **AU and CA disclosure:** the ACCC and the Competition Bureau both require clear, prominent, upfront disclosure of affiliate relationships.
- **Ontario insurance:** referral fees to unlicensed parties are restricted.
- **CASL:** cold B2B email to a conspicuously published business address is allowed only if the message is relevant to the recipient's business role and the publication carries no "no-spam" statement.

### Cited Findings: Australia
- **Credit referrer exemption.** A referrer may only (a) inform the consumer that a particular credit licensee or representative can provide credit activities, (b) say how to contact them, and (c) disclose any benefit or commission it may receive. It must not require the consumer to pay a fee. If outside the exemption, the referrer needs an Australian Credit Licence or appointment as a credit representative. — [ASIC FAQ: Does the credit legislation apply?](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-finance-professionals/credit-licensees/do-you-need-a-credit-licence/faqs-does-the-credit-legislation-apply); [Bright Law: scope of referrer exemption](https://www.brightlaw.com.au/scope-of-referrers-credit-licence-exemption/)
- **General advice.** ASIC RG 244 distinguishes factual information, general advice and personal advice. Giving general advice requires an AFS licence (or authorised-representative status) and a general advice warning under s949A(2). Licensing is covered in RG 36, advertising in RG 234 and design-and-distribution obligations in RG 274. ASIC has targeted "finfluencers" (2022 info sheet, which is older than 2024, flagged). — [ASIC RG 244 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/tkqi11il/rg244-published-13-december-2012-20211208.pdf); [ASIC RG 36 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/wdnk4aja/rg36-published-8-june-2016-20220328.pdf); [Clayton Utz: ASIC finfluencers (2022)](https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2022/march/asic-puts-finfluencers-on-notice)
- **ACCC disclosure.** Commercial relationships must be "clear, obvious and upfront … easily understood". Acceptable labels include "#ad", "Advertising", "Paid Partnership". "Affiliate", "#sp", "Collab" and "gifted" may be insufficient. Disclosure must be visible on first view and next to the content, not buried in hashtags. These come from ACCC influencer guidance as summarised by a law firm and a legal publisher. Read the ACCC's own "Guide for influencers" directly before relying on them. — [Legal123: ACCC guide summary](https://legal123.com.au/how-to-guide/legal-guide-social-media-influencers/); [Addisons: ACCC sweep](https://addisons.com/article/accc-announces-a-social-media-sweep-targeting-influencers/)

### Cited Findings: Canada
- **Ontario insurance licensing.** Selling, soliciting or negotiating insurance in Ontario requires a licence under the Insurance Act. FSRA has penalised unlicensed people who transmitted applications or charged fees (O. Reg. 347/07), and also the insurers or MGAs that paid them. — [Treadstone Law: Ontario regulated industries](https://treadstonelaw.ca/articles/regulated-licensed-industries-ontario-business-requirements); [Insurance Business: FSRA settlement](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/ca/news/breaking-news/fsra-settles-with-agent-who-impersonated-clients-on-calls-with-insurer-588272.aspx)
- FSRA has also consulted on an Unfair or Deceptive Acts or Practices (UDAP) rule for insurance. — [Stikeman Elliott](https://stikeman.com/en-ca/kh/insurance-law/ontario-s-fsra-consults-on-proposed-insurance-sector-rule-on-unfair-or-deceptive-acts-or-practices)
- **Competition Bureau.** The Competition Act requires disclosure of any "material connection" (including affiliate commissions), and the disclosure must be clear, prominent and close to the representation. Sources: the Deceptive Marketing Practices Digest Vol. 4, and Ad Standards' Influencer Marketing Disclosure Guidelines (updated 8 Oct 2025, with an affiliate-marketing section). — [Competition Bureau DMP Digest Vol. 4](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices-digest-volume-4); [Ad Standards Guidelines 2025 PDF](https://adstandards.ca/wp-content/uploads/Ad-Standards-Influencer-Marketing-Disclosure-Guidelines-2025-10-08.pdf); [BLG (2025)](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2025/07/trust-me-im-an-influencer-an-overview-of-influencer-marketing-in-canada)
- **CASL.** Every commercial electronic message needs consent, sender identification and an unsubscribe mechanism. Implied consent through "conspicuous publication" applies only if the address was published without a no-CEM statement and the message relates to the recipient's business role. The CRTC says this is "not a broad licence" and is assessed case by case. — [CRTC CASL FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm); [CRTC implied consent guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm)

### Inferences
- **Safe design pattern for both countries:**
  - Factual "Partner offers" listings with no product ranking or recommendation language.
  - An "Ad"/"Paid partnership" label next to each offer.
  - A commission disclosure sentence.
  - No collection of insurance or credit application data inside MileSprout.
  - Insurance and credit offers sent only to licensed marketplaces (RATESDOTCA, LowestRates.ca).
  - For AU credit and insurance, take legal advice before launch.
- **For outreach:** emailing a published affiliate or partnerships address about an affiliate deal fits the CASL conspicuous-publication test. The email still needs identification and an unsubscribe link. Australia's Spam Act 2003 has a similar "conspicuous publication" inferred-consent concept (not researched in this pass).
