## Q1. Which UK money partners pay the most per referral and accept app/mobile publishers?

### Takeaway
The largest published per-referral fees are in **business credit** (iwoca introducer fee of 3% of the loan amount; Capify "up to 10%"). These are broker/introducer programmes, though, and they bring FCA credit-broking issues (see Q3). Among **business accounts**, Revolut Business is reported at up to £500 [AGGREGATOR], Tide pays £50, and Starling was reported at £15 per funded business account (an older case study). Among **tax software**, Ember (£150 per converting lead) and TaxScouts/Taxfix (£25 flat) have explicit flat fees. Xero (10%), Sage (7%), QuickBooks (10% / small GBP bounties) and GoSimpleTax (10%) pay a percentage of the sale. I could not confirm, for any partner, whether in-app placement by a mobile app publisher is explicitly allowed, because the terms pages could not be fetched. Starling's programme does track app installs through Impact, which suggests it is set up for mobile.

## Q2. Which partners allow passing a reward to the user?

### Takeaway
Some programmes have a **user-side reward built in**: TaxScouts/Taxfix (10% off for the referred user), Pie Tax (£50 to the new user), and Tide's and PensionBee's refer-a-friend schemes (£50 each, though these are customer-referral schemes and may not allow commercial publishers). I found **no partner terms that explicitly allow or ban** a publisher-funded reward such as "12 months MileSprout Pro free when you open X". That has to be confirmed in writing in each programme agreement, because finance programmes on Awin, Impact and Partnerize often restrict "incentivised" or cashback traffic.

### Cited Findings
- TaxScouts: the referred customer gets 10% off a service — [taxscouts.com/affiliates](https://taxscouts.com/affiliates/)
- Pie Tax: ambassador and new user get £50 each — [pie.tax](https://www.pie.tax/tax-pible/pie-app-ambassador-program)
- Tide refer-a-friend: £50 each after the friend opens and spends £500 — [mikemcdonnell.co.uk](https://mikemcdonnell.co.uk/tide-referral-code) (blog, Aug 2026)
- PensionBee: £50 each on pension transfer — [MoneyGrower 2022](https://moneygrower.co.uk/2022/02/pensionbee-refer-a-friend/) (**possibly stale**)

### Inferences
- A "MileSprout Pro free" reward funded by MileSprout is an inducement. For **non-regulated** partners (e-money accounts, tax software) the issues are mainly the network or advertiser's incentivised-traffic rules and CAP Code fairness. For **regulated** products (deposits, credit, insurance, pensions), an inducement tied to opening the product forms part of the financial promotion. It must be fair, clear and not misleading, and it is a Consumer Duty matter for the product provider (see Q3). Expect providers to want sign-off.

### Gaps
- Explicit incentivised/cashback clauses for Tide, Revolut, Starling, Countingup, Ember, GoSimpleTax, Xero, Sage and QuickBooks were not retrievable.

## Q3. What exactly must MileSprout do to promote bank accounts, credit, insurance and pensions legally?

### Takeaway
s21 FSMA bans an unauthorised business (MileSprout) from communicating a financial promotion unless an authorised person has approved it or an exemption applies. Since **7 February 2024**, an authorised firm can only approve promotions for unauthorised persons if it holds the FCA's new **approver permission** (the "s21 gateway"). In practice MileSprout should only show **provider-supplied, provider-approved** creative. Each product type is treated differently:

| Product | Regulatory position for MileSprout |
|---|---|
| **E-money and payment accounts** (Tide, Revolut, Countingup, ANNA) | Not "controlled investments", so s21 does not apply. Normal CAP/consumer law still does. |
| **Bank deposit accounts** (Starling, Mettle, Monzo) | Can use the **FPO Article 22** deposits exemption for non-real-time communications, provided the required deposit-taker information accompanies them. |
| **Credit** | Needs an approved promotion. Introducing sole traders to regulated credit (generally under £25k) is **credit broking**, which needs FCA permission or appointed-representative status. A plain affiliate link carries risk here. |
| **Insurance (income protection) and pensions** | Need approved promotions. Introductions can amount to insurance distribution or arranging; the typical route is to become an **Introducer Appointed Representative (IAR)** of an authorised principal. |
| **Advertising disclosure (all categories)** | ASA/CAP requires ads to be obviously identifiable. Label affiliate placements "Ad". |

### Cited Findings
- An unauthorised person must not, in the course of business, communicate an invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity unless an authorised person has approved it or it is exempt — [FCA PERG 8](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg8)
- From 7 Feb 2024, authorised firms need FCA permission to approve promotions for unauthorised persons (s21 gateway; policy in PS23/13) — [FCA PS23/13](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps23-13.pdf); [FCA "Approving financial promotions"](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/financial-promotions-and-adverts/approving-financial-promotions); [FCA financial promotions data 2024](https://www.fca.org.uk/data/financial-promotions-data-2024)
- Approvers must notify the FCA of approvals, amendments and withdrawals in certain cases, and must have the competence and systems to do the job — [Kroll](https://www.kroll.com/en/publications/financial-compliance-regulation/financial-promotions-gateway); [HLC summary](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/uk-a-short-guide-to-the-fca-financial-promotion-gateway)
- Firms are responsible for how their affiliates communicate financial promotions and need monitoring and oversight. Unauthorised promoters such as influencers who communicate unapproved promotions may be committing a criminal offence — [FCA FG24/1 Finalised guidance on financial promotions on social media](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg24-1.pdf)
- FPO Article 22 exempts non-real-time communications relating to deposits (not structured deposits), provided they are accompanied by specified information about the deposit taker — [FPO 2005, legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2005/1529)
- Credit broking includes effecting an introduction of an individual who wants credit to a lender or another broker. This needs permission even when ancillary to the main business. Limited permission is available only where broking is secondary to a non-financial main business — [FCA PERG 2.8](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg2/perg2s8); [FCA consumer credit permissions: common misunderstandings](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/consumer-credit-information/consumer%20credit%20-%20understanding%20cc%20-%20permissions-web.pdf); [FCA principal firms with credit broking permissions: good and poor practice](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/good-and-poor-practice/principal-firms-credit-broking-permissions-good-and-poor-practice-areas-improvements)
- An **introducer appointed representative** is an AR whose appointment is limited to effecting introductions and distributing **non-real-time financial promotions** — [FCA Glossary: introducer appointed representative](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/glossary/G585.html); [FCA SUP 12](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/sup12)
- Insurance distribution guidance, including ARs — [FCA PERG 5](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg5); [PERG 5.13](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg5/perg5s13)
- Principal firms must oversee their ARs — [FCA principals and ARs: responsibilities](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/principals-appointed-representatives/responsibilities-how-to-oversee)
- Authorised firms remain responsible for business accepted from unauthorised introducers or lead generators — [FCA news story](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/news-stories/investment-advisers-responsibilities-accepting-business-unauthorised-introducers-lead-generators)
- Consumer Duty applies to firms throughout the distribution chain for retail products, including ARs — [Clifford Chance 2024](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/insurance-insights/2024/07/tightening-the-reins-the-fcareview-of-the-appointed-representative-regime-and-impact-on-the-gi-sector.html); [getregure](https://www.getregure.com/blog/what-is-fca-consumer-duty-for-insurance-brokers/) (secondary)
- ASA/CAP: CAP Code rule 2.1 requires ads to be obviously identifiable. Affiliate links count as advertising. Use "Ad"/"#ad" prominently, before the user engages. The ASA last updated its affiliate disclosure guidance in May 2023 (**possibly stale**). The CMA now has direct fining powers — [APMA](https://theapma.co.uk/what-the-latest-asa-rulings-mean-for-affiliate-marketing/); [RPC Winter 2025](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/winter-2025/cma-and-asa-publish-updated-influencer-guidance-on-social-media-endorsements/)

### Inferences (not legal advice; confirm with a regulatory lawyer)
1. **E-money and payment accounts** (Tide, Revolut Business, Countingup, ANNA): e-money and payment services do not appear among the FPO controlled investments listed in PERG 8, so s21 is probably not engaged. Promotions still need to be clear, fair and not misleading under CAP and consumer law. They must not imply FSCS protection, and should state that these are e-money accounts.
2. **Bank deposit accounts** (Starling, Mettle, Monzo): use the provider's own creative and include the Article 22 deposit-taker information. The provider's BCOBS and Consumer Duty obligations mean it will likely require copy approval anyway.
3. **Credit** (iwoca, Capify):
   - Unregulated business lending, such as loans above £25k for business purposes, merchant cash advances and limited companies, is outside consumer credit broking.
   - Sole-trader loans of £25k or under are regulated. MileSprout's audience (gig couriers, sole traders) falls mostly in that bracket. Being paid per loan introduction would then likely be credit broking, needing FCA permission or appointment as an AR.
   - Any promotion of regulated credit also needs s21 approval from a gateway-permitted approver, or must come directly from the authorised lender.
4. **Insurance and pensions:** these are controlled investments, so MileSprout needs provider-approved creative. Paid introductions may be regulated "arranging" or insurance distribution, so the safe route is IAR status under the provider or broker (e.g. Drewberry, Cura). Under an IAR appointment the principal takes on regulatory responsibility and oversight, and MileSprout can only introduce and distribute non-real-time promotions. A plain affiliate link with no AR appointment, using only provider-approved copy, is common practice. It is legally riskier for remunerated introductions, especially in insurance and pensions.
5. **Consumer Duty** applies to the authorised provider and any AR principal, not directly to an unauthorised affiliate. Expect providers to impose:
   - copy approval;
   - restrictions on incentives;
   - target-market matching (e.g. not pushing credit to financially vulnerable gig workers);
   - monitoring of MileSprout's placements.
6. **Practical checklist:**
   - Use only partner-supplied, approved creative and keep version records.
   - Label every placement "Ad".
   - Do not give personalised recommendations ("best account for you"), which could be advice.
   - Keep credit and insurance/pension partners out until an IAR/AR route or approver agreement exists.
   - Get written partner permission for any user reward.
   - Show risk and FSCS/e-money status as the provider requires.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact FPO exemptions for general (non-investment) insurance promotions, or the RAO exclusions that might cover a mere affiliate link for insurance or pensions. This needs PERG 5 and PERG 8 read in full, plus legal review.
- I found no FCA statement specifically about in-app affiliate placements by non-financial apps beyond FG24/1.
- The exact current CAP guidance URL for "affiliate marketing" on asa.org.uk was not retrieved; the secondary sources (APMA, RPC) are cited instead.
