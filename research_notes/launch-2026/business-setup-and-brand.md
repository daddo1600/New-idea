## 2. Sole trader or limited company

### Facts checked (2026/27)
- **Companies House online incorporation fee is £100.** It doubled from £50 on **1 February 2026**, so the £50 figure is out of date. Paper filing is £124 and the annual confirmation statement is £50 (up from £34).
- **Identity verification:** since 18 November 2025, every director must verify their identity (free through GOV.UK One Login, about 10–20 minutes) and get a personal code **before** the company can be incorporated.
- **Corporation tax:** 19% on profits up to £50k, 25% above £250k, and marginal relief in between (an effective rate of about 26.5% in that band).
- **Dividends from 6 April 2026:** 10.75% at the basic rate, 35.75% at the higher rate and 39.35% at the additional rate. Each basic and higher rate went up 2 points. The dividend allowance is £500.
- **VAT registration threshold:** £90,000 of taxable turnover over a rolling 12 months (deregistration at £88,000). It has not changed since April 2024 and stays the same for 2026/27.
- **Making Tax Digital for Income Tax** (sole traders only) started on 6 April 2026 for qualifying income above £50k. The threshold drops to £30k in April 2027 and £20k in April 2028. It means quarterly digital updates.

### Apple sells the subscriptions and handles the sales tax (confirmed from Apple's own agreement)
Apple's *Exhibits to Schedule 2 & 3* (dated 27 August 2026) say:
- **Exhibit A:**
  - **Canada:** Apple Canada Inc. sells as their agent.
  - **Australia:** Apple Pty Ltd sells as their agent.
  - **US:** Apple Inc. sells as their agent.
  - **UK:** Apple Distribution International Ltd sells as their commissionaire. The UK is in that section's country list, as best I could read it from the PDF.
- **Exhibit B:** "Apple shall collect and remit to the competent tax authorities" the relevant taxes in a list of countries that includes **Australia, Canada, the United Kingdom and the United States** (and all EU states).

So Apple collects and pays the VAT/GST/sales tax on App Store sales in all four launch markets.

**[Confirm with pro]** How their App Store income counts toward the UK £90k VAT threshold. Their supply is arguably to an Apple entity outside the UK. If so, it may be outside the scope of UK VAT and not count toward the threshold. Direct UK sales, such as a paid B2B pilot with the coffee brand or consultancy, would still count.

### Comparison
| | Sole trader | Ltd company |
|---|---|---|
| Set-up | Register for Self Assessment, which is free | £100 at Companies House, ID checks, a registered office (a service costs about £20–£60 a year **[Unverified]**), and PAYE if they pay themselves a salary |
| Tax | Income tax at 20/40/45% plus Class 4 NIC at 6%/2% on profit | Corporation tax at 19–25%, then dividend tax, plus optional salary |
| Liability | Unlimited personal liability | Limited, though directors still have duties and banks may want personal guarantees |
| Privacy | Home address can appear publicly. Under the EU DSA, an individual trader's address and phone are shown on the App Store. | Registered office and service addresses can be used instead |
| Credibility | Fine for most purposes | Better for B2B partners (the coffee pilot, accountant partners), the Apple seller name ("MileMint Ltd" rather than their personal name) and investors |
| Accountancy | About £150–£500 a year for a Self Assessment return **[Unverified market range]** | About £600–£1,500+ a year for statutory accounts, the CT600 and payroll **[Unverified market range]** |
| Ongoing admin | Low. MTD applies above £50k now and above £30k from April 2027. | Annual accounts, confirmation statement (£50), CT600, payroll and dividend paperwork |

**Rough example: £30k profit, no other income [Confirm with pro]**
- **Sole trader:** income tax about £3,486 plus Class 4 NIC about £1,046, so **about £4.5k** in total.
- **Ltd, salary £12,570 plus dividends:** employer NI about £1,136 (assuming the Employment Allowance is not available to a company whose only employee is a single director), corporation tax about £3,096 and dividend tax about £1,365, so **about £5.6k**, plus higher accountancy costs.

At low profits a company usually costs more in tax and admin. It starts to pay off once profits sit in the higher-rate band, or when profit is left in the company to reinvest. If they already have a job, their income tax band changes this sum completely.

**Practical view:** the main reasons to incorporate now are not tax. They are the seller name shown on the App Store, privacy, limited liability and credibility with partners. It is reasonable to launch as a sole trader and incorporate before taking on the coffee-brand pilot or any contracts. Each change of Apple account type adds friction (see section 3), so if they expect to incorporate within a few months, doing it **before** launch is cleaner.

## 3. Apple: moving from an individual to an organisation account

- **Conversion is supported.** Apple's "Updating your account information" page says an individual membership can be updated to an organisation membership. The request goes through the **"Migrate Individual Account"** form at developer.apple.com/contact/request/migrate-individual-account. They must be the **founder or co-founder**, give the organisation's **D-U-N-S Number** and may need to send business documents. Only the Account Holder can do this.
- **Organisation requirements:** a legal entity (sole-trader trading names are not accepted), a D-U-N-S number, authority to sign for the company, a **work email on the organisation's domain** and a **public website on that domain**.
- **D-U-N-S:** free from Dun & Bradstreet through Apple's look-up tool. Apple says D&B takes **up to 5 business days**, which cannot be sped up, and then up to 2 more business days to sync to Apple. A brand-new UK company may not be in D&B yet, in which case they requests a number through Apple's tool. The legal name must match exactly.
- **What changes:**
  - The seller and developer name on the App Store becomes the **legal entity name** (e.g. "MileMint Ltd").
  - Apple warns that changing the organisation name changes the vendor name on **all** their apps, **cannot be undone**, and **resets identifierForVendor** for existing users at their next update. This matters for analytics and attribution tools.
  - Because the account is converted rather than recreated, the app, bundle ID, reviews and subscribers should stay in place. **[Unverified]** Apple's page does not say explicitly that subscriptions continue; ask Apple Support to confirm.
- **Timescales:** these can be long. A forum post from 2026 describes a migration stuck since April 2026 that was blocking a release. They should **not** start a migration in the middle of launch week.
- **The alternative is an app transfer** to a new organisation account. Reviews, ratings and the bundle ID are kept, and auto-renewable subscriptions carry on, but they must share the app-specific shared secret. TestFlight must be fully cleared first, and Sign in with Apple and keychain sharing need extra steps. Users may have to log in again. It is more work than a conversion.
- **Small Business Program (15% commission):**
  - **Enrolment is not automatic.** They must apply as Account Holder after accepting the Paid Apps Agreement (Schedule 2) and must declare any associated developer accounts.
  - The 15% rate starts **15 days after the end of the fiscal month** in which Apple approves them. Apply **before** the first paid subscription is sold.
  - It applies to developers with up to US$1M proceeds in the previous calendar year, and to new developers.
  - After an app transfer, proceeds are counted across both the sending and receiving accounts.
  - **[Unverified]** Whether enrolment survives a conversion. Re-check the status in App Store Connect after migrating.
  - Separately, Apple's standard rule takes subscriptions down to 15% after a subscriber's first year anyway.

## 4. Protecting the brand

**UK trade mark (UKIPO):** fees went up on **1 April 2026** to **£205 online for the first class plus £60 for each extra class** (previously £170 and £50).

Suggested classes:
- **9:** downloadable mobile app software. Essential.
- **42:** SaaS / non-downloadable software. Recommended.
- **36:** only if they will describe the product as financial or tax services. Mileage logs for tax claims sit at the edge of this class, and it is where the "MINT" overlap is greatest.
- **39** (transport/route information) is optional.

Classes 9 and 42 together cost **£265**; adding 36 makes it **£325**.

UK timeline: examination usually takes a few weeks. The mark is then published with a 2-month opposition period. If nobody opposes, it typically registers in about **4–5 months** **[Typical; Unverified]**. The UKIPO "Right Start" option lets them pay half up front and check the examiner's report before paying the rest.

**Other countries, later and in this order:**
1. **US**, as a large market: USPTO base fee about US$350 per class since January 2025, plus surcharges for free-text descriptions **[Unverified]**.
2. **Australia:** about A$330 per class using the pick-list **[Unverified]**.
3. **Canada:** about CA$470 for the first class plus about CA$140 for each extra class **[Unverified]**.
4. **EU**, only if they launch there: €850 for one class, €50 for the second and €150 for each further class **[Unverified]**.

The **WIPO Madrid** system can extend the UK application to these countries in one filing (basic fee CHF 653 plus fees per country **[Unverified]**). If they file within **6 months** of the UK filing date, the other countries treat their application as filed on the UK date (Paris Convention priority), which is a reason to file in the UK early.

**Grab now (cheap):**
- Domains: milemint.com, .co.uk, .app and .io, plus mile-mint.com and getmilemint.com/milemintapp.com if .com is taken.
- Handles: @milemint (or @milemintapp) on Instagram, TikTok, X, Facebook, YouTube, Threads, Bluesky and LinkedIn.
- Note that @milemint.bsky.social and a "MileMint" YouTube channel already exist.

## 5. Admin for launch

- **Privacy policy:** **required** by the App Store, with a link in App Store Connect and in the app. The App Privacy "nutrition label" must be accurate even if data stays on the device. Remember third-party SDKs such as crash reporting, analytics and RevenueCat, which can make them a controller or processor of personal data.
- **Terms of use:** Apple's standard licence agreement (EULA) can be used. Subscription apps must show the price, length and auto-renewal terms, and link to Terms and Privacy on the paywall.
- **ICO data protection fee:** since 17 February 2025 it is **£52 (tier 1, micro)**, **£78 (tier 2)** and **£3,763 (tier 3)**.
  - It is owed by controllers processing personal data unless they are exempt. The exemptions include processing **only** for staff administration, marketing, or accounts and records, and processing with no computer at all.
  - **If the app truly keeps all data on the device and they receive none** (no accounts, analytics, crash logs or support inbox kept), they may not be processing users' data as a controller. Even then, customer and partner emails and a mailing list count as processing, although these may fall under the accounts-and-records or marketing exemptions.
  - **[Confirm with pro / ICO self-assessment tool]** Many advisers simply pay £52 as cheap insurance. The fine for not paying can reach £4,350.
- **UK GDPR with on-device data:**
  - Location trips are personal data even when stored only on the phone, but if nothing reaches their servers their obligations as controller are minimal.
  - The privacy policy should still explain the on-device processing, how location permission is used, any iCloud backup or sync (Apple holds that data), any export or email features, and any SDKs.
  - Ask for "Always" location permission only with a clear purpose string.
- **UK subscription law (DMCC Act):** the new subscription contracts rules (reminder notices, easy cancellation, cooling-off) are now expected in **January 2027**, having been brought forward from spring 2027. Note this for UK subscribers. **[Confirm with pro]** How the rules apply when Apple handles billing.
- **EU Digital Services Act trader status:** only applies if they distributes in the EU.
  - Every app on EU storefronts must declare whether its developer is a trader. A paid subscription app is almost certainly a trader.
  - A trader's **address, phone and email are shown publicly** on EU product pages. A company's address comes from D-U-N-S; an individual must enter an address or PO box. Documents are verified.
  - If they leave the EU out at launch (UK, US, CA and AU only), nothing is needed yet.
  - This is another reason to use a company with a registered office, rather than their home address, before launching in the EU.
- **Banking:** keep business money separate even as a sole trader. Options:
  - **Starling Business**: free, FSCS-protected, good for a Ltd.
  - **Tide**: free plan with 20p transfers after 5 free a month; it is e-money, not FSCS-protected.
  - **Monzo Business**: Lite is free, Pro is about £9 a month.
  - **Mettle** (NatWest): free.

  Apple pays out in GBP to a UK account. **[Check the current product line-ups. Fees are from 2026 comparison sites.]**
- **Insurance:**
  - **Professional indemnity** is useful mainly if they sign B2B contracts or gives figures that people rely on for tax. A mileage log that gets HMRC/IRS claims wrong could be argued as a loss.
  - **Cyber and public liability** are less important for an on-device app.
  - Micro tech PI policies typically cost about £100–£300 a year **[Unverified]**.
  - The coffee-brand pilot contract may require PI cover. Check before signing.
  - The app terms should include a clear disclaimer: "not tax advice; verify with HMRC/IRS/CRA/ATO rates".
