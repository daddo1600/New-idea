# Rename shortlist: replacing "MileMint"

Date: 2 Oct 2026 · Status: **desk screen only, not legal clearance**

**Why we're renaming:** Dawked Up Productions LLC already sells "MileMint - Mileage & Expenses" (App Store, v1.3.4) and owns milemint.app. "Mint" can't be the lead word any more. The new name has to fit the leaf mark, the green gradient (#0E9F6E→#053D2E), gold (#FACC15) and the plain, money-back tone.

## How each name was screened

| Check | Method | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| App Store | Web search for the exact name, "<name> app" and apps.apple.com | Search engines don't index every App Store listing, and the search was US-only. Before deciding, search the App Store on a phone in the UK, US, CA and AU. |
| Trademarks | Web search for "<name> trademark" (Justia, Trademarkia, USPTO pages that showed up) | No direct database queries were made to USPTO TESS, UKIPO, CIPO, IP Australia or EUIPO. |
| Domains | `getent hosts` on `<name>.app`, `.com`, `.co.uk`, `get<name>.com`, `<name>app.com` | No DNS answer means it **may** be unregistered. A domain can be registered and parked with no DNS. No WHOIS/RDAP lookups were made. |
| General web | Looked for an established company with the same name in money, cars or apps | |

As a check, the method returned an answer for `milemint.app` and none for a random made-up domain.

> **Before committing:** get a paid trademark clearance search (a firm or service such as Corsearch or CompuMark, or a trade-mark attorney) covering classes 9, 36 and 42 in the US, UK, CA, AU and EU, plus a common-law and App Store search. Only then buy domains and file. No search hits and no DNS answer do **not** mean a name is cleared.

Language note: every "Mile-" name keeps the syllable "MileMint" already had. That's fine across our markets. It's positive in Hindi/Urdu/Punjabi (*mile* ≈ "got/met") and Polish (*mile* ≈ "pleasantly"). Romanian *mile* means "pity/mercy", which is only mild. Italian *mille* and Portuguese *mil* mean "thousand", which is harmless.

Caveat: the "Mile+word" space is crowded (MileIQ, MileClear, MileWiz, MileAI, MileCatcher, Miles rewards app, MILE shopping app). Any Mile- name relies on its second word to stand out, so a trade-mark lawyer may see the "Mile" part as weak or descriptive.

### Suggested next step
Take #1–#4 to a quick 5-second test with 5–10 couriers (UK + one non-native-English group): say the name once, ask them to type it, ask what the app does. Then get a paid clearance search on the top two.
