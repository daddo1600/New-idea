# MileSprout pricing panel (simulation)

Date: 2 October 2026. Status: simulated panel, not real research. Inputs: only the product facts, competitor App Store facts (October 2026) and driver quotes in the brief. No web searches.

## 1. Top 5

- **Keep tracking unlimited and free. Do not cap drives.** Every competitor caps or locks tracking. "Unlimited, no account" is the one thing that made every persona pick MileSprout. A cap turns it into a cheaper MileIQ and kills the free base that Perks needs.
- **Price Pro at £3.99 a month or £29.99 a year** (US $4.99 / $37.99; CA C$6.99 / C$51.99; AU A$7.99 / A$57.99). In the panel, £3.99 got 10 firm "yes" answers. £5.99 got 6. Revenue per user was about the same, so the lower price wins on reach and goodwill.
- **Push the yearly plan, with a 14-day free trial on yearly only.** Pro's value peaks at tax time. Monthly payers join in January (or April, July) and cancel weeks later. A yearly plan at 7.5 months' price smooths that out. The trial wins over the cautious, high-value users (trades, people with an accountant).
- **Small moves, Free → wider, never narrower.** Add a short weekly recap and a watermarked sample report to Free, so people see Pro's value before tax time. Nothing moves from Free to Pro. Say publicly that tracking will never be capped.
- **Projected paid conversion: about 5–8% of active users paying at some point in a year** with option 1, against 3–5% for option 2. Option 3 converts more installs (about 6–10%) but loses roughly half the free base and risks one-star "bait and switch" reviews.

## 2. Method in brief

- 16 personas: 4 each in the UK, US, Canada and Australia, across the work types in the brief.
- Each persona saw five offers, A–E. A–D were the four competitors, anonymised. E was MileSprout, shown in turn as option 1, 2 or 3 ("if E cost this, what would you do?").
- Competitor features were shown only as the brief gives them: free-plan limit and price. Non-UK personas saw competitor UK prices converted at the brief's rates; real local prices were not checked. D (Gridwise) was shown at its US price and marked "not on the UK App Store".
- Local MileSprout prices (rate, rounded to .99):

| | Opt 1 monthly / yearly | Opt 2 monthly / yearly | Opt 3 monthly |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK | £3.99 / £29.99 | £5.99 / £44.99 | £4.99 |
| US | $4.99 / $37.99 | $7.99 / $56.99 | $5.99 |
| CA | C$6.99 / C$51.99 | C$9.99 / C$77.99 | C$8.99 |
| AU | A$7.99 / A$57.99 | A$11.99 / A$86.99 | A$9.99 |

- Pay scoring: Y = would pay; M = maybe; N = no.

### Who changed their mind in round 2, and why

- **Towards MileSprout (4):** Dave (no account, data stays on phone), Amrit (language), Carol and Sian (privacy made a weak "yes" firm).

- **Hesitant (2):** Élodie and Steve. An unknown brand made them want proof (a trial, a working export) before paying.
- **Nobody moved to a competitor.** The main reveal risk is trust in a new brand, not the competitors' appeal.

### From stated intent to a rough real conversion

Stated intent is far too high. The reasoning:

1. **Intent to action.** Assume roughly 1 in 5 stated "would pay" answers becomes a real purchase. This is a common rule of thumb, not a measured figure. Option 1: 69% → about 14%.
2. **Panel mix.** The panel over-represents heavy users and people with accountants (10 of 16 have a clear Pro need). A real install base will have more casual and low-earning users. Halve again: option 1 → about 7%.
3. **Seasonality.** Many payers pay only around tax time. So the share paying at any one time is lower than the share paying at some point in the year.

| Option | Paying at some point in a year (of active users) | Paying at any one time | Active free base after 30 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **5–8%** | 3–5% | Full (about 100%) |
| 2 | 3–5% | 2–3% | Full |
| 3 | 6–10% of installs | 5–8% | About half |

**Rough revenue per 1,000 installs per year (UK prices).** Option 1: 6.5% × about £22 blended (a mix of yearly plans and a few tax-time months) ≈ £1,400. Option 2: 4% × about £33 ≈ £1,300. Option 3: 8% × about £25 (5 months of £4.99) ≈ £2,000, but with roughly half the free base for Perks. On these guesses, option 3's subscription gain is about £600 per 1,000 installs. Perks only needs to earn about £1.20 a year per retained free user to cover it, before counting reviews and word of mouth.

## 5. Deliberation

**Growth and pricing strategist.**
- Conversion: "Option 3 converts best per install. But a cap is how every rival works, and drivers hate it. Our edge is that we don't."
- Word of mouth: "Jenna, Hannah and Kwame will never pay. They will tell 20 friends. In option 3 they leave in week one."
- Price: "£5.99 loses four firm payers for about the same revenue. Start at £3.99. Push the yearly plan hard."
- Seasonality: "Sell yearly from November to January. Show 'Your report is ready' in the app at each tax deadline."

**Driver advocate.**
- Drivers already say £8–£10 a month is too much and go back to spreadsheets. "£3.99 is under half of that. It reads as fair."
- Review risk: "Adding a cap later is a bait and switch. Couriers talk in groups. One post can sink the rating."
- Free/Pro line: "Tracking and totals must stay free for good. Employees like Sian and Carol need a log, not a business suite. Show them a sample so they know what they'd pay for."

**Sceptical investor.**
- "Fine, but Perks is unproven. If Perks fails, you've got a 5% conversion at £30 a year. That's thin."
- Seasonal churn: "Monthly at tax time means three payments a year. Yearly must be the default, or the numbers don't work."
- App Store: "Auto-renewing subscriptions must give ongoing value. Pro has weekly tax set-aside, recaps and MTD quarters, so that holds. Trials must say clearly what they cost after. Don't lean on dark patterns at renewal." (Rules quoted from memory; check the current App Store Review Guidelines, especially section 3.1.2, before launch.)
- Review risk: "Agreed. Changing Free later breaks trust with existing users and shows in reviews. Decide now. If you might ever cap, cap from day one. I'd rather you never did."
- Perks: "Perks must not touch the privacy promise. If an offer needs location or data shared, it breaks 'data never sold'."

**Where they agree.** No cap. £3.99 / £29.99. Yearly-first. Prove Perks before relying on it. Keep the privacy line clean.

## 6. Recommendation

**Cap or no cap: no cap.** Keep unlimited automatic tracking free, for good. Say so on the website and in the App Store listing. A later cap would read as a bait and switch.

**Pro price:**

| Country | Monthly | Yearly | Yearly as months of monthly |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK | £3.99 | £29.99 | 7.5 |
| US | $4.99 | $37.99 | 7.6 |
| Canada | C$6.99 | C$51.99 | 7.4 |
| Australia | A$7.99 | A$57.99 | 7.3 |

**Free trial: yes, 14 days, on the yearly plan only.** It wins the cautious, high-value users (Steve, Élodie). No trial on monthly, so people can't trial at tax time, export and leave without paying. Time trial prompts for each country's tax season.

**What moves between Free and Pro:**
- To Free: a **short weekly recap** (miles and money this week). Keeps people opening the app, which helps Perks. The full recap stays in Pro.
- To Free: a **watermarked sample PDF report** (for example, the last 7 days). Shows Pro's value before it's needed.
- Nothing moves from Free to Pro.
- Keep in Pro: itemised log and full PDF, exports, send to accountant, MTD quarters, tax set-aside, earnings by platform, Ask MileSprout, Siri & Shortcuts.

**Revisit** after one full tax season. Raise the price only for new users and only if yearly conversion holds. Never take features away from Free.

## 8. Caveats

- **This is a simulation, not real research.** The personas, their answers and the percentages are reasoned guesses. They show likely patterns, not facts.
- The panel is small (16) and skewed towards heavy users. The intent-to-action and mix adjustments are rules of thumb.
- Competitor features beyond free limits and price were not shown, so the blind round favours MileSprout.
- Non-UK competitor prices were converted, not checked. Check local listings.
- Not checked: which 10 languages are supported; current App Store rules; Perks revenue per user.
- **Validate with the founding testers:** show options 1 and 2 to separate groups. Ask what they'd pay and when. Then measure real yearly-plan uptake and trial-to-paid in the first tax season.
