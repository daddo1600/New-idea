# Coined-name exploration: should the app be a "Tobi" instead of a "Mile-something"?

Date: 2 Oct 2026 · Status: **desk screen plus a simulated panel. This is not legal clearance and not user research.**

The founder asked: *"OR do we make this app a different name all together like TOBI… a fun name people would say 'hey I use x app'… Uber, Rung etc."* This note brainstorms about 250 short coined names, screens them hard, runs a **simulated** 30-persona panel on 15 survivors plus Milesprout, and recommends a top 5.

**Milesprout is rejected** because *prout* means "fart" in French (Québécois too). It appears below only as the benchmark row.

**App languages, from the repo** (`milemint/src/i18n/locales`): en, es, pt-BR, fr, ro, pl, hi, pa, bn, zh-Hans. That is the real list. The brief's version (de, it, ur, no bn or zh) was out of date. Every name was checked against the ten real languages, and against Québécois French and Urdu as well, since Urdu speakers share the Hindi readings. Parts of words and endings were checked too, not just whole words.

---

## 0. Headline

- **Short names (3–4 letters) are effectively all gone.** Of 86 first-round names, 84 had a live `.com`, and most also had `.app`, `get<name>.com` and `.co.uk`. Every real word we tried (Pip, Penny, Kiwi, Nudge, Maple, Fennel, Nutmeg…) is already a fintech, an app or a big brand. To be ownable, a coined name needs **5–6 letters** and an unusual letter pattern.
- **Best coined name: Kilomi.** It got the most first-choice votes in the simulated panel (9 of 30), won in Canada and Australia, and is DNS-free on all five domains we checked. Its weaknesses: "kilo" means drugs in US slang, and the "Kil-" prefix already has mileage apps (Kilevo, Kilometa).
- **Best fit with the logo: Goldot.** The gold dot *is* the name. It scored highest on trust among the coined names. Its weaknesses: goldot.com is taken, "gold" reads as gold investing, and Goldot is a metal-detector brand (a different class of goods).
- **Panel's favourite for fun: Vroo.** It had the best recall and fun scores and won the US group. **The screen killed it:** Vroom, Inc. is a listed US car-finance company (car + finance is our space), and only vroo.app answers as possibly free.
- **Coined vs a descriptive name (Milesprout as proxy):** a coined name wins on word of mouth and spelling, and is easier to own as a trademark. It loses on "what does it do?". In the store, the title "Kilomi: Mileage Tracker" fixes that. In speech, nothing does except repetition ("Kilomi, the mileage app").

## 2. The simulated panel

**This panel is simulated.** The 30 personas are model-generated and extend the segments in `ui-panel-milemint-mileclear-mileiq.md`. Every quote is invented and every number is directional. Use it to choose what to test, not as evidence.

**Method (simulated):**
- Each persona heard 16 names read once (the 15 survivors plus Milesprout), in one of two random orders to blunt first- and last-position effects.
- **Recall:** the 3 names they remember.
- **Say and spell:** hear the name, then type it.
- **Word of mouth:** "Would you say 'I use X' to another driver?" (1–5)
- **Fun:** fun or likeable (1–5)
- **Trust:** would you trust it with money and tax? (1–5)
- **Needs explaining:** after hearing only the name, could they guess "mileage/money app"? (% who guessed right)
- **First choice:** the one they'd want on their phone.

**What the numbers say (simulated):**
- **Names ending "-omi"/"-ami" blur together.** Tilomi, Tallomi, Rodami and Rodomi were confused with each other and with Kilomi, and almost nobody recalled them. Only one name with that ending can survive, and Kilomi carried the hook ("kilo").

- **Fun and trust pull against each other.** Vroo was the most fun and the least trusted. Goldot and Milesprout were the most trusted.
- **Every coined name needs explaining.** The best coined name (Vroo, 43%) was still far behind Milesprout (73%) on guessing what the app does.

## 3. Expert recommendation

### Coined name vs Milesprout, honestly
- **Where Milesprout is better:** it explains itself. 73% guessed what it does, against 37% for Kilomi. It feels more trustworthy for tax (3.8 vs 3.5), and tradespeople and the accountant preferred it. **But it is dead:** *prout* = "fart" in French, and the "Mile-" part is weak and descriptive in a crowded field.
- **Where a coined name is better:**
  - Easier to own as a trademark. Kilomi is *suggestive* (km), not descriptive; Drumi and Rodomi are close to arbitrary in English.
  - Shorter, and easier to spell after hearing it (73% vs 57%).
  - More word of mouth (3.8 vs 3.0).
  - Survives a later move beyond mileage (expenses, shifts).
  - No "Mile-" pile-up in App Store search.
- **What a coined name costs:**
  - It needs explaining every time it's said aloud.
  - Trust starts lower for a money/tax app.
  - Early marketing must always pair it with a descriptor: "Kilomi, the mileage app", "Kilomi, mileage tracker".
- **How the App Store title closes the gap:** Apple indexes the title most heavily. `Kilomi: Mileage Tracker` (23 characters) keeps the head term exactly as `MileMint: Mileage Tracker` does, and the subtitle keeps "HMRC Log for Couriers & Trades" and the other locale subtitles unchanged. Store search loses nothing. The cost is only in speech and in un-captioned social posts, so the icon, the first screenshot and every video caption must say "mileage".

### Does the leaf/sprout mark with the gold dot still work?
- **Yes, with all five finalists.** An abstract name frees the mark to carry the meaning. The sprout says growth, the gold dot says car and money, and the road-stem says miles.
- A coined name leans on the mark *more*, so keep it.
- **Don't add a full mascot.** MileIQ already owns the cartoon car. A mascot would also need illustration in 10 locales, and it lowers trust for tax (Helen).
- **Do give the gold dot a little personality:** a blink when a drive is saved, a hop at milestones. This gets most of a character's word-of-mouth value at none of the cost.
- Fit by name:
  - **Goldot** turns the dot into the hero.
  - **Kilomi, Drumi and Rodomi** have letters that can carry the marks: gold i-dots in all three, and "o" wheels in Kilomi and Rodomi.
  - **Vroo** would have suited a character best, but it's out.
