# Fund free Pro with an opt-in Perks tab

MileSprout can realistically sign about a dozen UK partners before its October 2026 launch without any FCA permission. They fall into three groups. The first is e-money business accounts: **Tide £50 per onboarded member**, Revolut Business, Countingup. The second is Self Assessment and Making Tax Digital (MTD) tax software: **TaxScouts/Taxfix £25**, **Pie Tax £50**, GoSimpleTax 10%, Xero 10%. The third is non-insurance vehicle products: **Octopus Energy £50**, Nextbase dashcams at 8%, and MOT/servicing bookings through BookMyGarage and Kwik Fit. Most of these programmes run on Awin, Impact or Partnerize, and joining those networks is free or costs a £1 deposit.

The single most important finding concerns the model itself. **"Get Pro free when you sign up to a partner" is likely to be rejected by Apple**. Guideline 3.1.4 bans requiring users to "engage in advertising or marketing activities to unlock app functionality". Guideline 3.1.1 requires in-app purchase for unlocking features. Guideline 3.2.2(x) only blesses incentives for actions *inside* the app. The compliant version of the same idea is to make Pro free for everyone and fund it from an optional, clearly labelled "Perks" tab that unlocks nothing.

Insurance, breakdown cover, credit and pensions pay well but bring FCA rules on financial promotions, insurance distribution and credit broking. Leave them for phase 2, through an authorised partner. Two caveats apply to every figure in this report. First, **almost every payout figure comes from search-engine snippets or third-party aggregator directories**, because the research environment blocked direct access to partner websites. Second, publicly listed partnership contacts were found for only two companies. Treat every number below as a lead to verify on the live programme page, not as a quote. None of this is legal advice.

## The UK launch shortlist is led by tax tools and e-money accounts

The best launch partners are the ones that are relevant to a courier and do not need regulatory permission. **E-money accounts and tax software are not "controlled investments"**, so the FCA's financial promotion restriction (s21 of the Financial Services and Markets Act) does not bite. Ordinary advertising and consumer law still apply ([FCA PERG 8](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg8)). Tax software has a second advantage: the mileage log is exactly what a sole trader needs to fill in a tax return. That is the most natural "next step" MileSprout can offer, and HMRC's Making Tax Digital deadline in April 2026 is pushing sole traders towards digital tools. The Starling–Ember deal is a sign of the same demand ([Starling](https://www.starlingbank.com/news/starling-agrees-to-buy-ember/)).

Ember is the biggest caution in this category. Its referral terms advertise **£150 per converting lead** ([Ember referral terms](https://ember.co/legal/terms/referral)). However, Starling bought Ember in August 2025 and said Ember would be **exclusive to Starling customers in 2026** ([Starling](https://www.starlingbank.com/news/starling-agrees-to-buy-ember/); [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/starling-acquisition)). Treat that payout as probably obsolete until confirmed. Two more tax brands have changed hands: TaxScouts is now Taxfix UK, and Coconut was bought by GoSimpleTax in 2023 ([AccountingStack](https://accountingstack.co.uk/accounting-software/reviews/coconut/)).

## Apple's rules decide the business model, not the partners

The idea of "Pro is free if you sign up to a partner" collides with three App Store guidelines at once ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)):

1. **Guideline 3.1.1** says that to "unlock features or functionality within your app… you must use in-app purchase", and that apps "may not use their own mechanisms to unlock content or functionality".
2. **Guideline 3.1.4** says you "may not… require users to purchase unrelated products or engage in advertising or marketing activities to unlock app functionality". That is almost a word-for-word description of "open a Tide account to get Pro".
3. **Guideline 3.2.2(x)** allows apps to "incentivize users to take specific actions *within* apps (e.g. completing a level, watching an ad)". Opening a bank account happens outside the app, so this permission does not help.

Taken together, the rejection risk for partner-unlocked Pro is **high**.

The good news is that Apple explicitly allows the money to flow the other way. **Guideline 3.1.3(e)** covers "physical goods or services that will be consumed outside of the app", which includes bank accounts, tax filing, MOTs and insurance. Earning referral commission on these is ordinary affiliate business.

There are three compliant models, in order of preference:

- **Pro free for everyone, funded by partner revenue.** Partner offers sit in a separate "Perks" tab and unlock nothing. This matches the founder's goal exactly, and it is the cleanest story for App Review and for users.
- **Pro stays a paid subscription, and Perks are a separate tab.** Partner revenue lets you keep the price low.
- **Partner-paid rewards.** The partner gives the user a reward directly, such as Pie's £50, Octopus's £50 credit or TaxScouts' 10% off ([Pie](https://www.pie.tax/tax-pible/pie-app-ambassador-program); [Octopus](https://octopus.energy/current-account-terms-and-conditions/high-referrer-affiliate-terms-and-conditions/); [TaxScouts](https://taxscouts.com/affiliates/)). The reward is real, but it never touches MileSprout's own features.

Apple also sets some limits on how far this can go. Guideline 3.2.2(iii) rejects apps "designed predominantly for the display of ads", so mileage tracking must stay the core. Guideline 3.2.2(ix) imposes loan disclosure rules (APR, a 36% cap) if loans ever appear. Guideline 5.1.2(i) bans making any reward depend on switching on tracking, notifications or location ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

Privacy is where MileSprout can still win, as long as no partner code goes into the app. Apple defines tracking as linking your app's data with other companies' data for advertising or measurement. Apple also holds developers responsible for every third-party SDK in their app, even unused ones ([Apple User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/)). The privacy-preserving pattern works like this:

- The app creates a random, single-use reference on the phone.
- It adds that reference to a plain outbound affiliate link (Awin calls this a "clickref", Impact a "SubId").
- The reference is stored only on the device.
- No email address, advertising ID or partner SDK is ever sent.

Apple has not ruled explicitly on whether such a link counts as tracking, so this is a reasoned judgement rather than settled fact. It should let MileSprout keep a strong privacy label, which is worth confirming against Apple's App Privacy definitions. Offerwall SDKs such as BitLabs pay a revenue share, but they put third-party code in the app ([BitLabs iOS SDK](https://developer.bitlabs.ai/docs/ios-sdk-v3)). That breaks the privacy promise, so avoid them. Impact's mobile deep-linking is set up by the *advertiser*, so publishers "don't need to change a thing" ([Impact AppsFlyer guide](https://integrations.impact.com/integration-guides/for-brands/plugin-integrations/mmp-mobile-measurement/integrate-with-appsflyer)).

## Partners will want honesty about incentives and tight control of financial copy

**The biggest commercial catch is "incentivised traffic".** On the major networks, each advertiser decides whether it accepts cashback, loyalty or rewards publishers. CJ, for example, has advertisers list disallowed publisher types in their programme terms ([Junction by CJ](https://junction.cj.com/article/tag/topic-promotional-methods)). RAC's £20 rate is for "non-cashback and loyalty" affiliates, and MoneySuperMarket sets bespoke rates for cashback sites ([RAC](https://www.rac.co.uk/affiliates); [Affilitizer](https://www.affilitizer.com/programs/moneysupermarket.com)). Both imply that rewarding users is tolerated but **paid at a different, likely lower, rate**.

If MileSprout frames itself as "we fund free Pro from partner revenue" and does not offer per-user rewards, it may qualify as an ordinary content or app publisher. Be honest on every network application, because misclassification is a common cause of clawed-back commissions. **No partner's terms on in-app placement or user rewards could be read**, because the pages were blocked. Get written confirmation from each partner before launch.

Joining the networks is cheap. **Awin** charges a £1 deposit, refunded with your first payment, and pays GB publishers by bank transfer (BACS) from **£20** ([Awin Success Centre](https://success.awin.com/articles/en_US/Knowledge/Why-do-I-have-to-pay-the-sign-up-fee); [Awin GB FAQs](https://www.awin.com/gb/faqs)). Impact, Partnerize and Paid On Results cover most of the remaining UK list. One open question is whether these networks accept app-only publishers without a website. A simple marketing site with a privacy page and a "how we earn" page removes that risk.

The UK regulatory map splits cleanly by product. In every case below, use only partner-supplied creative, keep dated copies, and label every placement "Ad".

| Product | What MileSprout must do (UK) | Launch? |
|---|---|---|
| E-money accounts (Tide, Revolut, Countingup) | Not covered by s21. Must be clear, fair and not misleading. Don't imply FSCS deposit protection | **Yes** |
| Bank deposits (Starling) | Use the provider's creative plus the deposit-taker information required by the Article 22 exemption ([FPO 2005](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2005/1529)) | Yes, with provider sign-off |
| Tax software, dashcams, MOTs, energy, fuel cards | Ordinary advertising rules only | **Yes** |
| Insurance, including breakdown | Listing insurance information or ranked lists from which users can buy can count as **insurance distribution**. Unauthorised introducers generally need appointed-representative status, with the **Introducer Appointed Representative (IAR)** as the lightest version ([FCA PERG 5](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg5); [IAR definition](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/glossary/G585)) | Phase 2, via an IAR or a generic comparison-site link after legal review |
| Credit (iwoca, Capify, Zuto) | Introducing sole traders to loans of £25k or less is **credit broking** and needs FCA permission ([FCA PERG 2.8](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg2/perg2s8)). Promotions need a gateway-approved approver ([FCA PS23/13](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps23-13.pdf)) | **No**, not at launch |
| Pensions (PensionBee) | A controlled investment that needs an approved promotion ([FCA PERG 8](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/perg8)) | Phase 2 |

Two further points matter here. First, the FCA holds providers responsible for their affiliates and warns that unauthorised promoters of unapproved financial promotions may be committing a **criminal offence** ([FCA FG24/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg24-1.pdf)). Expect providers to demand copy approval, ongoing monitoring, and assurance that credit is not pushed at financially stretched couriers. Second, advertising rules (CAP Code rule 2.1) require ads to be "obviously identifiable", so label offers "Ad" up front. The CMA now has direct fining powers ([APMA](https://theapma.co.uk/what-the-latest-asa-rulings-mean-for-affiliate-marketing/); [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/winter-2025/cma-and-asa-publish-updated-influencer-guidance-on-social-media-endorsements/)).

Each country adds its own rules:

- **US.** The FTC's 2023 Endorsement Guides treat affiliate links as a "material connection" that must be disclosed "clearly and conspicuously" ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/07/26/2023-14795/guides-concerning-the-use-of-endorsements-and-testimonials-in-advertising)). Insurance referral fees to unlicensed parties are generally allowed only if the referrer doesn't discuss policy terms and isn't paid per sale. Many states cap the fee at a "de minimis" amount, typically under $50, and Tennessee caps it at $25 ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2024/02/19/761025.htm)). That favours per-lead marketplaces such as EverQuote over per-policy deals.
- **Australia.** Credit referrals must stay within ASIC's narrow **referrer exemption**: say a licensee can help, give their contact details, disclose your commission, and charge the user nothing ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-finance-professionals/credit-licensees/do-you-need-a-credit-licence/faqs-does-the-credit-legislation-apply)). Any recommendation of a financial or insurance product is "general advice", which needs a licence or authorised-representative status ([ASIC RG 244](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/tkqi11il/rg244-published-13-december-2012-20211208.pdf)). The ACCC expects labels such as "Advertising" or "#ad" on first view; "affiliate" alone may not be enough ([Legal123 summary](https://legal123.com.au/how-to-guide/legal-guide-social-media-influencers/)). Uber now sells its own Vero/XCover rideshare insurance in-app, which crowds out insurance referrals ([Uber AU](https://www.uber.com/au/en/blog/au-rideshare-insurance-product-launch-2026/)).
- **Canada.** Ontario requires a licence to sell, solicit or negotiate insurance, and FSRA has penalised both unlicensed intermediaries and the insurers who paid them ([Treadstone Law](https://treadstonelaw.ca/articles/regulated-licensed-industries-ontario-business-requirements)). Other provinces, including Québec's strict AMF regime, were not researched. The Competition Bureau requires clear, prominent disclosure placed close to the claim ([Competition Bureau](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices-digest-volume-4)).
- **CASL** (Canada's anti-spam law) governs outreach emails. Cold email to a *published* business address is acceptable only if it relates to the recipient's role and the page carries no "no unsolicited email" statement. Each message must identify the sender and include an unsubscribe link ([CRTC](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm)). UK and Australian email marketing rules were not researched, so for now use the programme forms rather than cold email.

## Competitors show offers alone rarely pay the bills

**No mileage tracker in this research has scaled on partner offers alone.** Every partner-funded model that worked combined several income streams or relied on huge distribution:

- **Gridwise** combines a benefits store, a paid Plus tier at $14.99 a month, and a business selling anonymised driver data to cities, investors and financial institutions ([Gridwise benefits](https://gridwise.io/benefits); [Gridwise privacy policy](https://gridwise.io/privacypolicy/)).
- **Stride** earned insurance broker commissions across about 140 gig platforms and still sold to a distributor, Integrity, in July 2026 ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/stride-health-coverage-easier-gig-economy); [Kirkland & Ellis](https://www.kirkland.com/news/press-release/2026/07/kirkland-advises-integrity-on-acquisition-of-stride-health)).
- **Catch** ran a free model on insurance commission, shut down in 2023, and returned as an insurance-only product ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/catch-health-insurance-gig-workers-relaunches/)).
- **Everlance** made its partnerships work the opposite way: Lyft pays for Everlance to be a perk in Lyft's driver rewards, with discounts rising to free for top-tier drivers ([Everlance](https://www.everlance.com/blog/everlance-lyft-partnership)).

The lesson is to treat affiliate revenue as one leg of the stool. Two other legs are worth pursuing:

- **Distribution deals.** A gig platform, a union or an accountant network pays for, or co-markets, MileSprout as a perk.
- **Embedded deals.** A bank offers MileSprout's mileage log to its sole-trader customers, as Hurdlr did with Nymbus and Locality Bank ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220929005012/en/Nymbus-Forms-Hurdlr-Partnership-Enabling-Business-Banking-Clients-to-Equip-Customers-with-Suite-of-Expense-Invoicing-and-Accounting-Capabilities)).

Gridwise's data sales give MileSprout a ready-made point of difference: "your trips never leave your phone and are never sold." Para shut down in 2024 after gig platforms sent cease-and-desist letters over its credential-based integrations ([Buck Hill Capital](https://www.buckhillcapital.com/gig-workers-are-learning-their-worth-with-the-para-app/)), which favours MileSprout's GPS-only approach. The market is moving towards free. GigU made its driver tools free across the US in 2026 ([PRWeb](https://prweb.com/releases/gigu-makes-its-driver-profit-tools-free-across-the-us-and-launches-on-iphone-for-the-first-time-302891180.html)), and MileIQ's premium price reportedly rose to $13.99 a month, though that comes from an unverified comparison blog ([Ridester](https://www.ridester.com/mileiq/)). That is the opening a free Pro tier can exploit.

The gig platforms' own perk lists double as a list of brands already paying to reach this audience. Deliveroo's UK rider perks include Shell, Three and Caffè Nero ([Deliveroo](https://rider.deliveroo.co.uk/perks)). Uber Pro UK includes the AA, bp pulse and Octopus Electroverse ([Uber UK](https://www.uber.com/gb/en/drive/rewards/)). Zego runs a dedicated Uber Pro promotion page ([Zego](https://www.zego.com/promotions/uber-pro-rewards/)). Those brands are proven buyers. MileSprout's perks need to *add to* what the platforms already give, not repeat it.

Set expectations on revenue honestly. Using unverified aggregator benchmarks (finance conversion of about 4–8%), an illustrative 10,000 monthly users, 5% clicking an offer, 5% converting and a $50 fee comes to **about $1,250 a month, or roughly $0.12 per user** ([wecantrack](https://wecantrack.com/insights/affiliate-program-performance-statistics/); [dollarpocket](https://www.dollarpocket.com/affiliate-marketing-benchmarks-2026/)). That is enough to cover modest running costs at scale, but not a salary at launch-scale user numbers. Tax season (January in the UK) and new-driver onboarding are when per-user value peaks.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The question is not "which partner unlocks Pro?" — Apple will not allow that. It is "which partners can pay for a free Pro?" In the UK, the answer at launch is the unregulated middle ground: tax tools timed for MTD and the January deadline, e-money business accounts, and everyday vehicle costs. The well-paid categories (insurance, breakdown, credit and pensions) are where the FCA, ASIC and the provincial insurance regulators all draw the same line, and those are a phase-2 project built on an authorised partner rather than an affiliate link.

Competitors suggest that affiliate fees alone will cover running costs but probably not a business. The more valuable long-term deals are the ones in which MileSprout's courier audience *is* the product to a bank, insurer or gig platform. Strategic buyers such as Starling, Motus and Integrity have already paid for exactly that audience. The privacy stance ("never sold") is both the brand and the pitch. The single most important next step is to verify every figure in this report on live programme pages, because the numbers here are leads, not contracts.
