# Top 10 iOS Apps to Build in 2026: Market-Scored

## Market intelligence behind the list

| Signal | What the data says | So what |
|---|---|---|
| Non-game spend overtook games | Non-gaming IAP revenue grew 21% YoY in 2025 and passed games for the first time. Apple subscription revenue is about 3× Google Play's. | iOS-first subscription apps are the right bet. |
| Category conversion | Business, Health & Fitness and Productivity convert best because users want long-term solutions. Business is about 76.5% subscription revenue. | Pick a "long-term need" category, not an impulse one. |
| Price and paywall | Higher-priced apps convert about 2× better (2.8% vs 1.4% median). Hard-paywall trials convert 10.7% vs 2.1% for freemium. 17–32-day trials convert at 42.5% median vs 25.5% for trials under 4 days. | Don't underprice. Use a long trial on annual. Gate the automation, not the basics. |
| ARPU leaders | Health & Fitness has the top 14-day ARPU ($0.44 median). AI apps and Health reach $0.63 by day 60, about 2× the overall median. | Health and AI-assisted utility monetise fastest. |
| Speed to revenue | Median time to $1K MRR is 32 days for games and 113 days for business apps. | Business apps are slower to start but stickier. Plan runway for that. |
| Unhappy users = opportunity | Negative-review share: meditation 86%, diet tracking 65%, period trackers 61%, parenting 59%, productivity 57%. | Paying users already want to switch. |
| Proof points | Cal AI (teen founders) reached about $40M ARR and was bought by MyFitnessPal (Dec 2025). Opal reached about $10M ARR with 11 people at $99.99/yr. MileIQ raised its price to $13.99/mo in 2026 and users are complaining. | Narrow, well-executed consumer utilities can be very large. Incumbent price hikes open doors. |
| Free on-device AI | iOS 26 **Foundation Models** give free, private, offline LLM calls (iPhone 15 Pro+). | AI features with no per-user token cost change indie unit economics. |
| Solo reality | The median solo app makes under $1K/mo. Niche apps that find fit usually reach $2–12K MRR within 6–12 months. | Specificity beats breadth. |

## Scoring rubric (1–5 each, max 35)

**D**emand · **W**illingness to pay · **C**ompetitor weakness · **B**uildability (solo, native) · **U**nit economics (AI/server cost) · **R**etention (repeat need) · **A**cquisition (ASO/organic/social reach)

## One-paragraph cases

1. **Mileage + receipt tax tracker.** Every drive is money: the IRS standard rate is about 70¢/mile and the UK HMRC rate is 45p/mile for the first 10,000 miles. The incumbents are weak: MileIQ went to $13.99/mo in 2026, Stride sits at around 3.0★ with reliability complaints, and Everlance charges $8.99/mo and is the one to beat. The app pays for itself visibly, which is easy to sell. It is fully native: CoreLocation visits, VisionKit receipts, and on-device Foundation Models to classify trips and expenses at no cost to us. *See the full recommendation in `03-recommendation.md`.*
2. **GLP-1 companion.** Tens of millions of people use these drugs and many need daily logging; Health has the highest ARPU. There's a strong TikTok/Reddit community for organic growth. The risks: MyFitnessPal (now owning Cal AI) and other big players are adding GLP-1 features, and medical-claim review is strict (no dosing advice).
3. **Menopause tracker.** Large, underserved, high-intent audience with strong emotional retention. Period trackers draw 61% negative reviews, so the category's incumbents are disliked. Organic growth comes through women's-health creators.
4. **App blocker.** Proven at scale (Opal about $10M ARR), but crowded, and you need Apple's FamilyControls entitlement. Pricing anger at the leader leaves an opening for a cheaper, kinder alternative.
5. **ADHD planner.** Passionate niche with good word of mouth, but users churn when routines lapse.
6. **Private AI journal.** Foundation Models make it cheap to run and privacy is a real selling point, but the space is crowded (Day One, Rosebud, Apple Journal is free).
7. **Pet health.** Pet spending keeps rising and pets make great social content, but willingness to pay is modest.
8. **AI calorie tracker.** Proven huge ($40M ARR Cal AI), but it's a red ocean now owned by MyFitnessPal, and you'd need heavy paid-UA spend.
9. **Home vault.** Low competition, but it's an infrequent need, so retention is weak.
10. **Trades quote/invoice.** The best fit for **paid upfront**, because pros pay for tools. It's a smaller audience and harder to reach through ASO.
